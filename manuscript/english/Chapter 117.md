# Chapter 117 — Final Round — Part 3

---

Dax rolled twice before his body stopped obeying its own momentum, and came to rest face up, his view filled with a starless sky, only the dirty glow of the stadium floodlights filtering through dust that hadn't finished settling yet.

It hurt. That, by itself, was news.

He got up slowly, one arm first, then the other, and spat out some blood before fully getting to his feet. Oliver's hit was still ringing through his chest, an echo that refused to fade as fast as the others had that night. He looked toward the exact spot where he'd been a second before — the kid had already turned his back, gone back to his own fight with Yegor, with neither the time nor the intention to chase him.

The rest of the field hadn't stopped to wait for him.

To his left, between two rows of seats torn loose and scattered like a giant had emptied a drawer, Lukas was still holding on to Pier, Riku and Nadia hammering the same arm without breaking his grip, Oliver and Yegor still tangled in their own exchange. Nothing settled. Everything half finished. Farther off, near the north goal, another exchange, another group, the metal shriek never quite stopping every time someone put a foot wrong on the debris. A dozen small wars, all indifferent to the one he'd just had.

Dax dragged the back of his hand across his mouth, leaving a red streak on his skin, and took a deep breath. His whole body was asking for one more second, just one, before he decided where to move next.

He didn't get it.

"Dax."

The voice came from behind him, calm, with no need to rise above the noise to still be heard. Shouting wasn't necessary when volume wasn't what carried it.

He turned, and there she was, crossing what was left of the field with the same steady stride Dax had seen her use in the pit weeks earlier, the first time they'd measured each other without knowing yet what they'd become to one another.

Johanna Richter. The air around her wasn't just air anymore — something in it had thickened, a pressure Dax felt against his skin before he'd even fully seen her, the kind of thing no Domain could fake or imitate. She didn't need one. She never had.

"Johanna," he said, and something like a smile, tired but real, crossed his split face. "You took your time."

"I was busy," she answered, not hurrying her pace, not needing to. "Same as you, from the look of it."

She stopped a few meters off, her posture relaxed in a way Dax recognized, and knew from his own experience meant nothing calm at all. Behind her, the rest of the field kept burning in small, scattered fires of violence, but in the space between the two of them, something different had already closed off — a bubble neither of them had needed to ask for out loud.

"All week, avoiding this," Dax said, straightening fully, pushing the pain in his chest somewhere it wasn't allowed to matter. "Why now?"

"Because there's no one left standing between the two of us anymore," Johanna said, and for the first time something in her voice hardened, the calm from before turning into something else, something older.

"Looks that way," he admitted.

"Then let's settle the account."

There was no warning, none of the usual tell Dax read in almost everyone who came at him. Johanna simply closed the distance, the first strike already in the air before her own sentence had finished settling, a fist loaded with raw Odem that Dax blocked with his forearm on pure instinct — the impact traveled up the bone whole, a dull vibration he felt down to his teeth.

He answered without thinking, a straight cross that she dodged by turning her body just enough, and from there the exchange settled into a rhythm neither of them was fully improvising — strike, block, answer, each reading the other with the familiarity of someone who'd trained, without quite knowing it, for this exact moment.

Johanna wasn't using any Domain — she didn't need one. Every strike carried an amount of Odem behind it that Dax felt crash against his own reserve like a wave against a wall, nothing but raw quantity, no technique refining it. Dax took a hook to the side that pushed him back half a step.

"Strong," he said, almost approving, his voice still steady despite the pain.

"Always have been," she answered, without smiling.

The third exchange was the closest match yet. Both struck almost at the same time, fists meeting halfway in a collision that kicked up a visible ring of dust around them, and neither gave real ground. Dax felt the bone of his own forearm protest under the impact, and from the flicker that crossed Johanna's face, he knew it hadn't cost her any less.

They broke apart a step, both breathing faster than either would have liked to admit.

That was when Dax decided testing the waters wasn't enough anymore.

He shifted modes, the tell so subtle only someone who'd spent weeks watching him up close could have caught it in time — and Johanna, who'd done exactly that, caught it. Something in her stance adjusted a fraction of a second before Dax's next strike arrived, no longer loaded with brute force but with something else, his focus narrowing onto her and only her, the rest of the field vanishing from his sight as if it had never existed.

The Hunt.

The blow barely grazed her — the back of his hand against the forearm she raised to block, a contact that shouldn't have meant anything — and yet Johanna wavered for an instant, something draining out of her in a way no strike before it had managed. Not pain. Something else.

"What..." she started to say, and didn't finish the question.

Dax didn't answer. He couldn't, not in that mode, not with his focus already fixed on the next contact. He went back to the same point — a second graze, a third — each one carrying off another fraction of the monstrous reserve that had carried her through the whole night without ever fully running dry. Until tonight.

Johanna let the next blow land, and in the same instant she felt something drain out of her, she closed her fingers around Dax's wrist instead of pulling back — she knew, from the pit, exactly how much margin she had left, and it wasn't this yet. She closed the distance on purpose. At that range there was no tell to read, no stance to anticipate — only elbow, knee, raw weight, a close, ugly exchange where neither of them could afford to catch their breath.

Dax felt Johanna's knee drive into his side and, instead of answering with another strike, closed his arms around her and sank his teeth into the side of her neck — he didn't fully decide it; the mode already had the reins on that part of him. Johanna's whole body jolted, a short, hoarse scream that never quite finished, and the taste of blood reached Dax along with the other thing — the usual thing — now with nothing left to hold it back.

"Let go of me," she managed, her voice broken, fighting for air. "Dax!"

"Almost there," he answered against her skin, and it didn't sound like a threat. It sounded closer to an apology he never got to finish saying.

It wasn't a strike that beat her. It was time. Johanna twisted against the hold, the muscles of her neck and arms standing out under her skin with an effort that shouldn't have been necessary against a simple embrace, the veins of her forearm bulging as she tried, and tried again, to break free by pure strength. She dug her nails into his arm, hunting for any point that might loosen the hold. It didn't work. The color drained from her face with every second that passed, from the flush of effort to something paler, grayer, until her last attempt was nothing more than a tremor of her fingers against Dax's back.

Dax pulled back all at once, as if something had burned him, and for the first time that night it wasn't pain that closed off his stomach. It was the taste still in his mouth, the sudden clarity he'd spent half his life dreading, back again, uninvited. Real nausea. His hands were shaking, and not from exertion. He watched Johanna's body drop to the grass, the dark mark already visible on her neck, and couldn't let himself look at her for more than a second.

---

The whole stadium had gone, all at once, strangely empty of noise — the last scattered fights had died out one by one without Oliver seeing any of them end, absorbed as he'd been in his own. Now there was only the floodlights and the dust settling slowly over the ruined grass.

Oliver crossed what was left of the field with steps that no longer bothered pretending to hurry, each one heavier than the last, and stopped ten meters from where Dax still stood, his breathing still ragged, someone else's blood still on his mouth.

Dax looked him up and down, jaw still clenched, something behind his eyes Oliver couldn't quite name — not the glow from before, which had already faded, but not fully back to being just Dax either.

"All week, circling around this," he said, his voice hoarser than usual. "Time to finish it."

"Couldn't agree more," Oliver confirmed.

Dax ran his tongue over his split lip, an almost absent gesture, and something in his stance began shifting slowly — shoulders dropping, weight redistributing between his legs, the same stillness Oliver had first seen weeks earlier, in the pit, before he understood what it meant.

Oliver felt the Registry open on its own, unasked, reading every micro-adjustment as if Dax's whole body were a map he already knew by heart and still couldn't fully anticipate.

Something like a smile crossed Dax's face.

"Then let's not waste any more time on this."

Wind crossed the whole field, lifting fine dust out of the craters Yegor's Arsenal had left behind, and for a second the only thing moving between them was that — dirt suspended in the air, drifting down slowly under the dirty light of the floodlights.

Dax took the first step.

Ten meters, closed almost instantly, and Dax's shape loomed over Oliver mid-kick. Oliver read it instantly. Swap, and their two positions traded places. Dax's leg struck the dirt. A blast, and dust filled the air.

Both figures shot off in opposite directions, Dax's body rolling over itself until it landed on all fours like an animal, his face twisted with pain. Both of them leapt at the same instant.

They met in the middle, mid-swing on a hook from Oliver. Dax turned his body slightly, and by a single centimeter, managed to slip inside Oliver's guard. Oliver's strike grazed his temple, loaded with something more than Odem; Dax's blow landed clean, straight to the jaw. Both of them snapped backward, the momentum loaded into Oliver's strike releasing at the barest contact. Their heads swung like punching bags, snapping back and returning with the same resistance.

Dax used the last hook to wrap Oliver up in an arm hold, but Oliver saw it coming, slid one leg between Dax's two and redirected the momentum to take him down. Dax let go of the hold. Both of them rolled across the dirt for a couple of seconds, and Oliver was the first back on his feet.

He used Anchor right then, Dax's body frozen for a second — long enough for Oliver to close in and land a strike. Oliver's fist buried itself in Dax's face, driving his body into the dirt. Almost a cheap shot. He loaded a second strike. If winning meant grinding him down even on the ground, he thought, so be it.

He let the strike fall, charged with Odem, and mid-swing felt Dax's own weight land under his jaw — the man's arm turned to a blur. He was thrown several meters into the air.

It didn't end there. Dax found him again in midair, driven by something that felt more animal than human. He grabbed him by both shoulders, drove a knee into his solar plexus, and finished it by clasping both fists together, sending him crashing to the ground in a single blow.

Oliver lay there a moment longer than he wanted to, his back against churned-up dirt, the starless sky spinning slowly above him. The knee had left his chest empty of air, a dull pressure that wouldn't let go, and when he tried to breathe deep something pulled wrong in a rib, a dry stab that cut his breath off halfway. He braced one arm, then the other, and his whole body protested at once under the weight. The hit to his temple was still ringing, a dull echo that blurred the edge of his vision every time he turned his head too fast.

When Oliver looked up, Dax was still there, breathing ragged, more worn down than he'd expected.

"Come on! I know you've still got more in you — I can taste it," he said, the same smile as before.

Oliver reached for words, too exhausted to get any of them out. He checked inside himself and could only confirm the obvious. He was empty — whatever reserve he had left, he was already spending just to stay standing. Instead, he raised his guard and sharpened the Registry, something behind his eyes drilling into the back of his skull.

"That's it, that's it!" Dax shouted, his natural calm clouded the way water clouds when something breaks its surface.

Dax moved first, slower this time.

A hook, loaded with his whole body, aimed at the face. Oliver ducked under it. Dax came back with an elbow to the shoulder, and Oliver spun with the momentum, the arm passing clean over his chest. Dax reset his footing, turned his body, and swept with his leg. Oliver jumped, landing with a movement that struck Dax as oddly familiar. A kick that Dax deflected with his hand — weaker, but identical to the one that had opened the fight. Dax threw his arm back, hoping to land something even by luck, and missed. Oliver hopped back, opening the distance a couple of steps, his eyes fixed on Dax, his mind somewhere far from there, his body moving almost on its own momentum. And Dax wondered if he was copying him on purpose.

He came in again with a jab that turned out to be a feint. Oliver's body pulled back, and Dax released the real strike — a hook from the opposite side. It missed by mere centimeters, the kid's body moving with more agility than his condition should have allowed.

Oliver barely thought about it — he threw a hook, loaded with his whole body, straight at Dax's face, and Dax ducked under it. Oliver came back with an elbow to the shoulder, and Dax spun with it, the arm passing clean over his chest — the same reaction, the same angle, as if he were watching himself a second earlier. Oliver reset his footing, turned, and swept with his leg. Dax jumped, and something in his face changed mid-fall, recognition arriving late. He countered with a kick in midair that Oliver barely deflected with his hand. Oliver threw his arm back, hoping to land something even by luck, and as Dax dodged it, he recognized it — the exact same pattern, his own movements. He closed with a strike straight to the face that buckled the kid's leg, and went for a second to finish it — and mid-swing, too late already, he realized. Oliver's arm had turned to a blur.

Dax was thrown several meters into the air.

It didn't end there. Driven by something that felt more animal than human, Oliver found him in midair. Oliver's arms closed around his shoulders, and Dax guarded his stomach, anticipating the knee — which grazed past his defense instead, straight to the face.

"What..." Dax started to say, his voice different from a moment before, and didn't finish the question.

His vision clouded instantly, and he felt the world go black for a moment — a moment Oliver used to finish it, clasping both fists together and sending him crashing to the ground.

Dax lay there, cheek against the churned-up dirt, the darkness retreating slowly from the edges of his vision. Pain wasn't the first thing he registered on the way back. It was the cold certainty that he'd just lost to his own moves, returned to him with a precision he'd never given them. For an instant, something foreign crossed his face — no instinct, no appetite, only the bare calculation of someone measuring a distance before deciding whether it's worth crossing. One second, two. Then it passed, and he was just Dax again, jaw clenched, his whole body demanding he get up before his head had even finished clearing.

There were no words this time. None were needed.

Both of them charged at once, the distance between them vanishing in a single step each, everything they had left riding on that last exchange.

In that instant, a voice cut across the stadium.
