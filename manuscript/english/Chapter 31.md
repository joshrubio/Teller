# Chapter 31 — Escalation

Cartwright left that same afternoon, without saying where.

There was no formal goodbye — Park heard about it from Ferreira, who took a two-minute call and hung up with the same expression he greeted almost everything with.

"He's gone," he said, pocketing the phone. "Says he trusts the team's judgment. He'll be back when he's needed."

"Is that normal?"

"Nothing that man does is normal. But yes, for him, this is close to routine." Ferreira was already putting on his jacket. "Filipa found something. Let's go see it before her mood for explaining it twice runs out."

Dona Filipa received them without looking up from the table, three folders open in front of her at different points along a timeline Park couldn't read upside down.

"The symbol isn't new to the world," she said, no preamble, her voice dry in the way of someone who'd spent the whole morning talking to nothing but documents. "It's new to Lisbon. I found two cross-references in another branch's correspondence, fourteen years back. A brief incident, closed fast, not much detail in the summary that made it to us."

"Which branch?" Park asked.

"The letterhead's cut off. Deliberately, I think." Filipa finally looked up, and something in her gaze carried the weight of someone who'd learned not to be surprised by what the institution hides. "But the phrasing pattern is Eastern European. It matches what Salgado said about the signature's accent."

Bruno, who'd spent the last half hour fixing the broken device at the next table, didn't lift his head but did speak.

"Fourteen years, and nobody connected it to anything until a symbol showed up in an Alfama courtyard."

"Because nobody had a reason to connect it," Costa said, no edge to it, the kind of correction that happens between people who've worked together for years. "Until yesterday."

Ferreira kept staring at the folder a moment longer than necessary.

"We need to confirm this in person," he said. "Not with instruments. With people."

---

They found the first one before noon.

It wasn't hard — Ferreira knew the city's underworld well enough to know who to ask, and the answer came faster than Park expected: a man, around thirty, lived in an apartment over a record shop in Mouraria, and hadn't left it in three weeks.

The door opened before Ferreira had finished knocking.

"I already told the others I don't want to talk about this."

"What others?" Ferreira asked, not forcing the door, not stepping in.

The man — Park never learned his name, and something about the way he stayed in the shadow of the hallway suggested he preferred it that way — went quiet a beat too long.

"Doesn't matter." He started closing the door.

Ferreira put his hand on the frame, no force behind it, just presence.

"We're not here to do anything to you. We're here because someone else might be going through the same thing you are, and we need to understand what 'this' is before it happens to somebody who doesn't know how to survive it."

The man looked between the two of them, weighing it, and something about the word "survive" seemed to loosen something in him.

He let them in.

The apartment smelled shut-in, curtains drawn in the middle of the day. The man sat on the edge of a couch without inviting them to do the same, his hands laced together too tightly.

"Three weeks ago I started feeling things I shouldn't feel," he said, unprompted. "Weight on my chest when there was nothing near me. Things moving when I wanted them to move, before I touched them." He laughed, no humor in it. "I thought I was losing my mind. Went to a doctor. The doctor didn't find anything, because there was nothing a regular doctor could find."

"How did it start, exactly?" Park asked.

"A man. Foreign, though his Portuguese was almost perfect. Stopped me on the street, said something about my... " he searched for the word, "potential. Touched my shoulder. That's all. A second, maybe two."

Park felt the weight of the air in the room shift, though nothing in the room had changed. He looked at Ferreira, who'd already caught the same signal.

"Can you show us?" Ferreira asked, with a gentleness Park hadn't heard from him before.

The man reached a hand toward the low table in front of the couch, without touching it. The coffee cup sitting there, gone cold, shuddered, slid two centimeters, and stopped.

"No training, no control," Ferreira said, more to himself than to the man. "Just the gift, raw, on top of someone who never asked to have it."

"Can you help him?" Park asked.

Ferreira stepped closer, slowly, hands open so the man could see he was carrying nothing. He closed his eyes a moment, and something in the air of the room shifted in a different way — not the foreign weight of a freshly awakened gift, but something finer, almost imperceptible, as if Ferreira were pulling on a thread only he could see.

"I can't take it away," he said, opening his eyes. "Nobody can. But I can show you where the knot slips loose every time you try to control it." He pointed at a spot in the air, level with the man's chest, that was nothing to Park but that the man looked at as if he could suddenly see it too. "There. When you feel something wanting to move, squeeze there, not outward."

The man closed his eyes, concentrated, and the cup slid back to its original position, slower, more controlled.

It was the first time in the whole visit that he smiled for real.

"Come see us again," Ferreira said, standing. "This doesn't get solved in an afternoon, but you don't have to solve it alone."

The man nodded, and something in his shoulders dropped a degree, as if he'd spent three weeks carrying the weight alone and had just discovered he didn't have to keep doing that.

"How many more like you are there in the city?" Ferreira finally asked.

"I don't know. I know I'm not the only one. There's a group, people who find each other, who help each other because nobody else is going to." He looked straight at Ferreira. "A woman organizes it. I don't know her full name."

Park didn't need it confirmed. Neither did Ferreira.

---

Back on the street, neither of them spoke until they'd walked half a block.

"This is what Salgado couldn't confirm with instruments," Ferreira said finally. "It's not an isolated incident. Someone is waking people up at random, in this city, and not even bothering to do it carefully."

"Why would someone do that?"

"That's the question I still don't have an answer to." Ferreira stopped at the corner, looking into the maze of Mouraria opening in every direction. "What I do know is that if this has been going on long enough for an organized support group to exist, it's not an incident anymore. It's a pattern. And patterns have an origin."

Ferreira's phone rang before he could finish the thought out loud. He answered, listened for five seconds, and his expression changed in a way Park was already starting to recognize as a bad sign.

"Vitória," he said, hanging up. "Says there's someone at the office. Someone who shouldn't have been able to get in without anyone seeing him arrive."

---

When they got there, the man was already standing in the middle of the main room, as if he'd been there his whole life and not a few minutes.

Tall, taller than Ferreira, with a calm that didn't match being surrounded by four armed agents, none of whom dared get too close. Vitória had her hand near her weapon, Costa the same, Bruno had put himself between him and the equipment table without anyone asking him to.

"Nobody saw him come in," Vitória said, not taking her eyes off him. "The hallway cameras show nothing between the two checks."

The man smiled, the kind of smile that didn't reach the eyes.

"Forgive the lack of an announcement," he said, with an accent Park couldn't quite place, something Central European softened by years of speaking other languages. "I came to introduce myself before someone else did it for me, with less courtesy."

"Who are you?" Ferreira asked, not moving from the doorway.

"A colleague, in a sense. From a different branch, with a problem similar to yours." He looked straight at Park, appraising him with the same attention Mariana had used on him the night before, but with none of her care behind it. "And you must be the agent from Korea. I'd heard Cartwright was playing his game again, placing pieces in interesting spots."

Park didn't answer. The air in the room carried the same weight he'd felt in the Alfama courtyard — not ambient this time, but a single point, standing in front of him, smiling.

"Did you leave the mark in the courtyard?" Park asked.

"I was the one who wanted it found. There's a difference." He took a step toward the map table, and none of the four agents dared fully block him. "I only came to say that what you're investigating is about to get harder than your branch is prepared to handle on its own. And that I'd prefer Dona Mariana not get in the way when that happens."

"Why tell us that?" Ferreira asked. "Why not just disappear, the way you did at the courtyard?"

"Because I wanted to see a new face before deciding whether he's worth worrying about." He looked back at Park, the smile a degree colder. "And I've decided."

He turned toward the exit, and Bruno, on instinct or the accumulated frustration of the whole morning, moved to block his path.

Ferreira shouted something Park didn't fully process before it happened.

The man didn't touch Bruno. He simply passed close, and something left him in that same instant — not a blow, a thread of Odem Park couldn't see but felt pass by, weight concentrated in a point so small that neither he nor anyone else in the room had time to get in the way.

Ferreira tried anyway.

In the fraction of a second between the man's gesture and the moment it reached Bruno, Ferreira closed his hand in the air, the way you'd catch a loose thread — and something gave, deflected a degree, not enough to stop it entirely but enough that it didn't land whole. Bruno dropped to his knees with a cry that wasn't physical pain, something deeper, something that drained the color out of his face in a second. Costa caught him before he hit the floor.

It was less bad than it should have been. Park knew that from the way Ferreira stood there staring at his own hand, breathing faster than normal, as if he'd just held something far heavier than his Domain was built to hold.

By the time Park reached the spot where the man had been standing, he was already gone. The main door, locked from the inside, was still locked.

Ferreira knelt beside Bruno, who was breathing but not responding to his name, eyes open and fixed on something that wasn't in the room.

"What did he do to him?" Park asked.

"The same thing he did to the man in Mouraria," Ferreira said, not looking up, the hand he'd used to deflect the strike still shaking a degree. "Except this one didn't get three weeks to adjust. And I could only take away a fraction of it. I don't know if it was enough."

Vitória was already calling for an ambulance on the office phone, her voice controlled but fast. Costa didn't let go of Bruno, holding his head so it wouldn't hit the floor every time his body tensed on its own.

Park stood there, nothing useful to do with his hands, processing what he'd just seen the only way he knew how: in pieces. A man had walked in and out of a locked building without anyone seeing him. He'd woken a stranger on the street with a second's touch. He'd dropped a trained agent without even touching him, and he'd done it while looking Park in the eye, deciding whether it was worth the trouble.

*I've decided,* he'd said.

He hadn't said what he'd decided. Park didn't need him to explain it to understand the answer wasn't good for anyone in that room.
