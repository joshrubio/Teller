# Chapter 108 — Hunter and Prey

---

"Hiding is a tactic of the body," Anka had said, weeks earlier, in a windowless room in the massif. "A wall, a distance. Void is something else: shutting off the entire signature for anyone tracking you through Odem. The cost is that once you're off, there's nothing else you can turn on — no Registry, no Domain — until you come out of Void. And coming out has one instant of total vulnerability, before you get any other advantage back."

At the time it had sounded like a theory exercise, one more technique on a list of four. Crouched by a park wall in Milan, his pulse still high from the run there, Oliver understood for the first time that Anka hadn't been teaching a class. She'd been giving a survival instruction weeks ahead of schedule.

"We're not the only spectators," Oliver whispered.

Riku turned his head so fast that for an instant all Oliver saw was the back of his neck, tense, searching the dark for something he himself hadn't pinpointed yet.

"Where?"

Oliver narrowed his eyes, letting the Registry settle over the whole clearing instead of just Dax. And he saw it, further back, between two trees about fifteen meters east of the clearing, something that didn't fit: an outline too still to be a branch, too warm to be a stone. And another, further south, crouched behind a toppled bench.

"There," he said, tipping his chin barely. "And there. Not moving."

"More of Iker's crowd?" Riku asked, his voice thinned to a thread.

"I don't know." Oliver counted a third silhouette, then a fourth, scattered with no pattern that suggested a team, each keeping its own distance from the others. "If they're Iker's, they're not acting like his people."

The fight had already started by the time the answer arrived on its own.

Dax had closed the distance with the first of the two nameless ones before the kid finished raising his arms — a short strike to the plexus that folded him forward, and Dax used that same fold to take him down with the shoulder, wasting no second movement on something the first one had already settled. The kid didn't get up. He was breathing, but badly, a sharp whistle every time his chest tried to fully expand.

The silhouettes to the east and south didn't move an inch.

"They should be moving in by now," Riku said, the certainty rising in tone even as the volume stayed low. "If they're Iker's, this was supposed to be everyone closing in together. No one just stands there watching their own finishing blow get lost."

"Unless they're not expecting to win," Oliver said, the idea taking shape as he said it. "They're waiting for someone to get tired."

Riku didn't answer right away. When he did, his voice came out even lower, as if saying it out loud made it truer than he wanted.

"Like the ones that circle a wounded herd. They don't hunt. They wait for the hunt to already be done, and take what's left."

The second of Iker's group went for Dax's right flank, taking advantage of the exact second he still had his weight forward over the first one. Dax pivoted on his left heel — not away from the blow, into it, closing the angle before the fist fully extended — and intercepted it with his forearm. The impact sounded wrong, bone against bone with no flesh in between to absorb it, and the kid staggered back, cradling his wrist against his chest.

Iker still hadn't moved. He watched from the north edge of the clearing, arms loose at his sides, weight distributed evenly between both feet — not the stance of someone hesitating, but of someone waiting for the exact moment.

The third came from behind, and that was when something in Dax changed.

There was no transformation, no flash, nothing an untrained eye could have named. But Oliver, with the Registry still open, saw the structure reorganize — Dax's awareness losing the periphery all at once, narrowing onto the kid approaching from behind as if the rest of the clearing had stopped existing. His stance dropped a degree, his shoulders a little further forward, his breathing long and even where a second before it had been short and fast.

Dax turned and landed an elbow along the sternum before the third one completed the grab he was going for. The kid didn't scream. The air left him all at once, he dropped to his knees with his eyes wide and nothing coming out of his mouth, and an instant later Dax had already stepped back, his gaze still fixed on him one second too long, as if he needed to confirm something before letting it go completely.

"Did you see that?" Riku whispered.

"I saw something change," Oliver said. "I don't know what."

The fourth didn't even try. He stayed where he was, eight paces from Iker, and that stillness was all the information Oliver needed: it wasn't lack of will. It was already the lack of anyone willing to be next to test it.

---

Dax had seen them before Oliver finished counting the first silhouette among the trees.

Not with his eyes — with something older, a weight in the air to the east and south that didn't match anyone he already had in front of him, the exact same feeling he'd spent half his life learning not to ignore. Four still points, keeping their distance, no intention of moving yet. Scavengers, he thought, without needing the full word to recognize the pattern — waiting for him to tire before deciding whether it was worth coming closer.

That could wait. What was in front of him couldn't.

Iker was still at the north edge of the clearing, not having moved an inch from the start, and for the first time that night Dax felt the real weight of who he was about to fight — not one of the two nameless ones, already on the ground or close to it, but the only one of the five who'd spent months studying him up close without Dax realizing how much.

"Finally going for real," Iker said, opening the channel for Static with his arms. His feet spread his weight, his stance closing the same way it had in that isolated room at St. Gotthard, when he'd faced Riku.

---

The idea reached Oliver almost before Iker finished the sentence.

He pulled off his belt without fully thinking it through, wrapped it once around each hand, and pulled it taut between them until the leather stopped giving.

"Can you kill communications?" he whispered. "The whole park."

Riku looked at him as if the question needed translating twice before it made sense, and when the translation arrived, Oliver saw the whole shift cross his face — first something like excitement, almost a smile that never fully formed, and then, right behind it, real fear.

"Oliver."

"They're both in the middle," Oliver said, no longer needing to lower his voice further, each word carrying the weight it needed to. "Dax and Iker. No one left out there is better than those two. If we move now, anyone we run into is going to be weaker than us."

"You fought Dax once," Riku said, slowly, understanding before he finished saying it. "And you think that gives you margin against anyone who isn't him."

Oliver didn't deny it.

Riku didn't answer right away. He knew he could do it — it wasn't theoretical, he'd already practiced it, a field wide enough to drown any short-range frequency in a radius of a whole block — and he also knew, with the same clarity, what using it meant: no communicator, no way to call for help, no way for Asha or Nadia to know where they were or what had happened to them until they reappeared themselves.

"If I do this, we're really on our own," he said. "No turning back halfway."

"I know."

Riku closed his eyes for a second, and when he opened them his fingers were already extended into the air, the skin on his forearms prickling before anything visible happened. Oliver's earpiece spat out a dry crackle and died completely.

Oliver was already moving before Riku finished.

"Oliver!"

There was no time to grab him. Riku swore under his breath, something short and irritated, and followed anyway, reluctantly, still feeling like he'd just agreed to something he shouldn't have.

---

Dax hadn't had time to process the crackle of static that reached him from beyond the clearing — something in the air changed, one frequency fewer, though he couldn't say which — because Iker was already on him.

The first exchange was pure testing: Iker threw a short field toward the chest, not to damage, to read how Dax responded to pressure. Dax absorbed it with a crossed forearm, without stepping back, and there was the data Iker needed — he was still reading the gesture, not the full vector behind it. Iker's second strike went to the same point, but this time truly loaded, and this time Dax felt it: an electric whip that ran up the bone of his arm and numbed his fingers for a full instant.

Dax stepped back two paces, shaking his hand, and for the first time that night Iker smiled.

"There it is," he said. "That's real."

He switched modes again — Instinct, this time, his gaze opening back up to the whole clearing instead of narrowing on a single point — and went straight in, without the previous testing detour. He loaded the entire strike of Static into his fist and released it against Dax's chest in the exact instant Dax was completing his counterattack turn, giving him no time to redirect anything. Dax flew backward into a tree trunk that groaned without fully splitting. Bark torn away, splinters falling. The lowest branch broke off and hit the ground a meter from where Dax was getting back up, slower than the time before.

The whole clearing no longer looked like the one Oliver had seen when he walked in.

---

Oliver approached the first silhouette to the east still in Void, no more noise than the grass giving under his exact weight, and for one full instant — the instant Anka had warned him would never fully disappear — he stopped being fully invisible before the blow landed.

The kid caught sight of him, eyes going wide, half a second before the belt crossed his throat from behind.

He didn't drop clean. He fought back, hands scrabbling for the leather, and Oliver felt the whole pull of the struggle run up his arms — he loaded Momentum into the grip without thinking, the same reflex trained a thousand times in the secondary room, and the kid lost his balance all at once, yanked backward by a force that didn't match the size of whoever was applying it.

That was when Riku, finally reaching the exact spot where he'd seen them struggling, found no one.

---

Oliver reappeared against the trunk at the far end of the clearing, twenty meters west, the kid still tangled in the belt and completely disoriented — the Swap hadn't given him time to understand what had just happened to him before the two bodies collided with a third one waiting crouched right there.

The three of them rolled through the roots, coming apart by their own momentum, and when Oliver got back up it was the second one who was in front of him — not a stranger. He recognized the face before the name: someone from Johanna's group, from St. Gotthard, though not one of the two or three she'd taken with her that same morning.

Neither of them said anything. They charged each other almost simultaneously, Focus concentrated in the fist, a first clash that sounded like bone against bone and settled nothing. The second exchange was faster — Oliver read the feint toward the shoulder, that wasn't the real attack, and shifted the angle just in time for the real blow to graze his jaw instead of landing full.

The ground started moving before either of them completed a third exchange.

It wasn't a footstep tremor. It was something deeper, a wave rising from under the disturbed earth, and Oliver understood, a second too late to be cheap, that it wasn't coming from the kid in front of him. It was coming from the first one — the one from the belt and the throat, still on the ground among the roots a few meters off, hands sunk into the earth up to the wrist and his face twisted into something that wasn't pain.

The ground didn't tremble. It burst.

The blow caught Oliver in the side, a dull, whole impact he never saw coming in time to do anything about, and the force sent him flying several meters toward the center of the clearing, where he landed rolling over churned dirt and loose roots.

"Give it back!" the first one shouted, still on his knees, his voice cracked with effort.

Oliver didn't get up right away. He lay there a moment, breath scraping, and smiled anyway — three badges hanging from his neck, his own and two more, gleaming against the churned earth under the only light still standing.

The second one reached for his chest, feeling for his own, and found nothing.

An instant later, whoever was getting up in front of them was no longer Oliver.

It was someone else entirely — disoriented, blinking against the same broken light, not yet understanding why the ground under his feet had just stopped existing.

---

Riku had lost the trail between two identical rows of trees, the Static field still held around him in case someone else's comm trail gave him away, when he felt the change.

It wasn't a sound. It was the field itself, a tiny interference crossing the air to his left at a speed nothing else in that park should have, and his body reacted before his mind finished processing why. He stepped half a pace aside.

Where he'd been standing an instant before, still vibrating, stood an arrow made of something translucent that was neither glass nor metal — condensed Odem, he understood, without needing the Registry to know it.

"Close," a woman's voice said, between two trees ahead, not fully stepping into the light. "But not close enough."

Riku raised his guard, loading Static into hands and feet at once, the same closed stance he'd learned from Iker. He never got to use it.

The grip fell on him from behind with no warning at all — an arm thick as a beam closing over his chest, lifting him half a foot off the ground, and Riku fought against something that weighed and held like it was part of the ground itself. Two seconds, three, gaining nothing.

And then the grip stopped pushing.

It didn't let go — it simply stopped moving, frozen at half-pressure, as if the whole arm had turned into the same kind of thing it was holding. Riku didn't stop to understand why. He slipped down and to one side, just in time to see the second arrow bury itself in the shoulder of the man who a second before had had him trapped.

The man didn't even scream. He stayed there, fixed, unable to react even to his own pain.

A third arrow was already coming when Riku, still on his knees, saw Oliver crossing between the trees toward him at full speed, and understood — Anchor. He'd pinned the man in place like just another object, exactly long enough for the girl's arrows to find another target.

A curt nod told him the rest of the plan without a word.

Riku got to his feet and ran toward him. A flicker — barely that, a fraction of a second of something too visible crossing the air between them — and whoever was in front of him was no longer Oliver.

It was the girl with the arrows, disoriented, still half-drawing the bow between her hands.

Riku didn't decide the punch. The adrenaline released it before the decision finished forming, a full hook loaded into her ribs, and the girl's body shook far more than he expected — she fell sideways, convulsed once, and didn't move again.

Riku turned, still with his fist closed, and found Oliver standing over the body of the man who seconds before had had him grabbed, still fixed in place, the pain from his pierced shoulder with no way out.

"Quick," Oliver said. "On his neck."

---

They ran out of the park long before their bodies finished processing what they'd just done, laughter coming out almost without permission, the kind of laughter that has nothing to do with anything being funny.

"Four badges," Riku said, breathless, shaking his head as if repeating it would make it more real. "Four. I can't believe it. I can't believe it."

"You didn't do too badly yourself," Oliver said, and the comment came out with a lightness he hadn't felt in weeks.

"A hook. I threw a hook at a girl who was shooting solid Odem arrows at us and I didn't even decide it, my hands decided for me—"

They were still laughing when they came out onto the street, and there it cut off all at once.

Asha and Dara were waiting on the sidewalk across, still, not having had to look for them. Asha's look needed no words behind it — it already said everything.
