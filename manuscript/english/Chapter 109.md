# Chapter 109 — Located

---

Elena Krauss had spent three days saying the trail felt different, and it had taken Park those same three days to understand she was right.

Not weaker. Older — as if someone had stopped smoothing it out halfway through. Elena described it with her hands, fingers spreading over the map unfolded on the car's hood: a density disturbance that usually arrived smooth, almost polished, now edged, grainy, the kind of ragged texture a Domain leaves behind when whoever's using it is too tired, or too rushed, to do it right.

Fifteen days earlier, the trail had led them to an empty apartment hours before they arrived. Ten days earlier, to a freight platform and to Sofia with a shard of metal buried in her arm. This time the pattern was different — not a clean jump to another city, but three separate addresses inside the same eight-block radius, as if Lev and Sabine no longer had the energy to run far, only to keep moving inside a space that kept shrinking.

"He's failing to hide," Elena said. "Or he doesn't care anymore."

Marc didn't look up from the map.

"More than a month on the run, there's no difference between the two."

Sofia, the scar still visible under her left sleeve, was the first to say it out loud.

"They're tired. Really tired."

No one contradicted her. Sofia had been back on active duty for eight days since the platform — the container fragment had left a pink line that still pulled when she extended her arm fully, and a new habit, quieter, of noticing things she used to let pass. She hadn't talked again about the exact moment Lev released the blast, but Park had seen her, more than once, looking at her own hands as if checking they were still whole.

"Three days of the trail getting worse, and no sign they've asked anyone for help," Elena said, folding the map. "They're really alone. No network, no Aurelio, no one else left to burn."

"That makes them more predictable," Marc said. "Or more dangerous. You never know which until you test it."

Park stayed out of that discussion. He'd spent three days running a different tally, one he didn't say out loud in front of the team: more than a month on the run, two people who hadn't hurt anyone on purpose in all that time, a file that still called them a Level 2 risk as if none of that mattered.

Park didn't answer right away. He was looking at the point Elena had just marked on the map — a three-story building over a canal, windows dark except one, in a neighborhood that no longer bothered pretending to be a place anyone just passed through. The institution in him wanted to call it a confirmed target. The part of him that these last few months had learned to call Oliver something more than an assigned case kept calling this, too, by another name he hadn't found yet.

---

They moved at dusk, the team split into two approaches — Marc and Sofia via the service stairwell, Park and Elena along the canal, the curtainless third-floor window their only real point of reference, some twenty-five meters ahead.

Park was the first to see Lev's silhouette cross in front of the light, and something in his stomach clenched before his head finished processing the recognition. More than a month of trail suddenly turned into a real body, thinner than the file described, moving with the specific exhaustion of someone who hadn't slept a full cycle in a long time.

"Confirmed," Elena said into the earpiece, her voice stripped to essentials. "Both of them."

The fire escape gave them away before Park and Elena reached the canal door — Sabine already on the landing, her head turning toward the sound of Marc a second before he finished leaning out, and the recognition was instant on both sides. No shout. Sabine touched Lev's arm once, wordless, and the two of them moved, not toward the open street, but toward the canal itself — the only direction that didn't have anyone in uniform yet.

Park went after them along the stone edge, closing the distance slowly despite the real effort it cost him — Lev carried more than a month of flight in his legs, not the speed of someone rested, and he was still gaining ground with every stride. Forty meters. Thirty. The canal narrowed up ahead, a bend where the water turned against a retaining wall taller than a man, and Park understood, before they did, that there was no way out there — only when Lev and Sabine reached the bend and stopped short did they understand the same thing.

They ended up less than ten meters away, in a real dead end, the canal's own retaining wall closing off the way forward.

There they were. Both of them, backs to the water, with nowhere left to go.

Lev turned first, hands already open at his sides, the air around his fingers starting to warp with heat pulled from something Park couldn't identify yet. Sabine barely moved at all — feet planted, weight low, the same stance Park had seen in the simulation files, pure containment, ready to pin down anything that came too close.

"You don't need to do this," Park said, hands visible, not moving any closer. "It's over. We have you."

No one moved for a full second.

It was Sabine who spoke, no plea at all in her voice.

"And now what? You take us back to a center that doesn't exist for us the same way anymore? Or to something else you haven't told us yet?"

"I don't know," Park said, and it was the only thing he said that night he hadn't rehearsed before saying it.

Lev hadn't taken his eyes off him the whole time, the heat still gathering slowly in the air between his fingers, never quite released.

"We knew it would be you, sooner or later," he said, not as a question. "Pier always said you were the type who didn't give up on a case half-finished."

"Pier shouldn't have said that out loud."

"He didn't say it thinking of this," Sabine said. "He said it thinking of Oliver. We were just listening."

Park had no answer ready. He looked at Lev, the heat still gathering in the air between his hands without releasing, and felt the exact same cold clarity from the second before he'd struck the Báthory Shackle instead of the body — knowing where to aim, already knowing, before impact, that the result wouldn't look any different from an execution. He had the angle. Marc and Sofia sealed the exit behind them. The canal wall sealed the one ahead. One call, one gesture, and this ended right there.

The file said Lev and Sabine were a containment escape, Level 2 risk, standard resolution.

What Park had in front of him was two exhausted people, no real hostility in their bodies, looking at him the same way Oliver had looked at him less than a year earlier — not like an enemy, but like the last variable before something got decided without them getting a vote.

He had the whole angle. One gesture, one different word, and this resolved itself without anyone else ever knowing — no clean report, no capture, two names that simply didn't turn up that night. He felt the entire possibility, concrete, within reach, for exactly as long as it took him to decide not to take it.

"Elena," he said, his voice giving away nothing of what he'd just ruled out. "Contact confirmed. We proceed per protocol."

"Park."

"We proceed."

Sabine still didn't move, eyes fixed on him, as if she'd seen something cross his face a second before the order came out and wanted to ask why it hadn't stayed there.

"Last words, none," Park said, taking the first step toward them. "Come without making it worse."

It was Lev who moved first, not toward Park, but toward the retaining wall itself — palm open against the concrete, the whole afternoon's accumulated heat released all at once into the stone instead of into a person. The wall didn't shatter. It frosted over matte white in a second, and the sound it made giving way wasn't an explosion — it was one long sigh, like something that had been holding itself up for a long time and had finally stopped. The gap it left, barely wide enough for a body, opened straight onto the canal.

Sabine had already thrown herself through behind him before Park finished understanding what he'd just seen.

"Marc, the water!" Park shouted, already running, but the shout came too late — Marc and Sofia were on the wrong side of the building, the service stairwell a real thirty seconds away, and thirty seconds, that night, was exactly what they didn't have.

Park reached the canal's edge in time to see two silhouettes swimming away toward the far bank, too fast for the exhaustion he himself had seen in them minutes before — the kind of strength that only shows up when there's no other real option left. He didn't fire. It wouldn't have changed anything at that distance, and some part of him, one he didn't want to examine too closely, was glad to have the excuse.

Elena reached his side, out of breath, watching the water.

"They're gone."

"I can see that."

Marc and Sofia arrived a moment later, the service stairwell still echoing behind them. Marc looked at the gap in the wall, then at Park, without saying anything yet — the kind of silence that on another team would have been an accusation, and on this one was, for now, only an unasked question.

"Could we have caught them?" Sofia asked, quieter than usual for her.

"Not swimming, not with what we had on us," Elena said. "Not without risking something worse than two tired fugitives."

Sofia didn't push, but Park felt her gaze linger a second too long on him, not on the water — the same look she'd spent eight days learning to use since the platform, the one that no longer accepted an explanation just because it came from above.

"And now?"

Park didn't answer right away. He stood looking at the smoking gap in the wall, the frost already melting at the edges, and thought, unable to help it, of Sisi crossing the north wing toward Kaspar, her hand closed around Tomás's wrist, and of the exact window he himself had had that night to intervene and hadn't used. The same question from back then, still unresolved, now with a new layer on top of it: he'd chosen protocol, not the easy way out, and the result had been exactly the same. He'd never know now what would have happened if he'd actually had to hold the other decision through to the end.

"Report the contact exactly as it happened, no embellishment," he said finally. "And let's go after them."

He stood by the water a moment longer, not calling anyone yet, feeling the exact weight of what he'd just chosen not to decide.

Behind him, the team waited in silence for him to move first — Marc packing away the map without folding it, Sofia's hand still near the scar without touching it, Elena watching the smoking gap like someone searching it for an answer the wall itself was never going to give. No one asked anything else. Park took the first step toward the car, and the other three followed, each of them carrying their own version of the same nameless question.
