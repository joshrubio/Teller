# Chapter 116 — Final Round — Part 2

---

A harsh, grating sound rolled down from high in the stands — like an entire instrument snapping all its strings at once, carrying something that wasn't music and had no interest in becoming any.

"DAAAX!" Yegor's scream arrived a second later, from the exact spot where he'd landed after flying backward, wind churning around him under the pressure of his own Odem — too much of it, all at once, for a single body to hold.

Oliver caught it first with the Registry, and only after that with his eyes — an amount of Odem spreading over the entire stands like water covering sand, faster than anything Yegor had shown all night. A roar climbed up behind it: hundreds of pieces of metal bending and giving way at once, folding seats and stretches of railing tearing loose from their anchors around the whole perimeter of the stadium.

That was when Oliver understood what was about to happen.

"No," slipped out of him, one foot already a step ahead of the other, mid-stride toward nowhere useful.

There was no time to run. Hundreds of seats, chunks of railing, loose pieces of the structure itself lifted off the stands all at once, and an instant later started raining down across the whole field like a storm Yegor himself was steering — every fragment charged, every one with a destination of its own.

Oliver didn't think.

He triggered Swap against someone from Johanna's group, the one closest to the densest part of the fall, and the two of them traded places without a sound — she, an instant earlier standing dead center in the rain of debris, appeared on the retaining wall where Oliver had been standing a second before. Before the gap the swap left behind had finished closing, Oliver's eyes were already on the next target: one of Yegor's recruits, frozen under a whole row of seats falling straight for him. Oliver pulled him out the same way — the boy reappeared ten meters off, unhurt, with no time to understand what had just saved him.

A third swap, a fourth, a fifth — Oliver lost track of who he was moving and where, left with only the cold certainty that every Swap cost more time than he had. And then, without meaning to, he did it. Ten, twelve, maybe more, all at once. Bodies traded for debris, sent as far away as he could manage.

It wasn't enough for everyone.

The rain of metal crashed down across the whole field, hundreds of dull and sharp impacts blurring into one continuous roar, and what was left of the grass vanished under debris, bodies, and screams that no longer sorted themselves by side.

Yegor closed the distance, his body launched like a projectile, fist charged, Dax the target. When the two bodies collided, a shockwave scoured the air clean of the dust the metal rain had kicked up seconds earlier. Dax stood untouched, arms crossed in a gesture that looked, to Oliver, like it had cost him some real effort.

He wasn't the only one.

In the middle of the collision, a force Oliver recognized instantly shoved both bodies toward the ground, trying to crush them both flat. It was Pier.

"NOW!" The voice was hoarse, torn from somewhere deep in his throat.

Instantly, Asha and Riku leapt, one from each side. Their bodies charged with Odem for a second, and the weight over the area vanished — the exact instant their strikes landed, clean, on both targets. Yegor's body flew off, tracing a curve through the air. Dax's didn't.

Asha's expression changed faster than her stance did, her jaw clenching. The fist — faster than she could have anticipated — a hook, and the crackle came through the cloud of dust.

The hook landed right on the liver, charged with electricity. It was Riku. Dax's body jolted for an instant, his expression darkening with pain. Out of the corner of his eye he caught the dark-haired young man — terror flooding his face despite the hit landing clean.

Dax smiled, and whatever should have frozen him for a couple of seconds turned out not to be enough.

The same strike he'd charged a second earlier for the enemy in front of him turned into something else. Something closer to a whip caught Riku — Dax's arm blurred, struck him with the back of his hand, Odem focused into the exact point of contact — and Riku's body went flying into the grass, tumbling several meters before momentum finished its work. Enough of an opening for Asha to land a second strike against his jaw, a third to the body, a fourth to the forearm, a fifth straight at the man's hand, a sixth at the free one. Both his arms pinned.

"Well played," Dax said, slamming his forehead into hers, the strength draining out of her body all at once.

Asha went down without finishing the seventh strike, the hand that had been reaching for Dax's wrist an instant before going slack against the grass.

---

Oliver watched all of it from the edge of the crater the metal rain had left behind, and he thought about it no more than he'd thought about any of the Swaps before it.

He ran for the center, arriving just as Nadia did the same from the opposite side — Impulse in a straight line, straight for Dax's side, no angle, no feint, only the hope that speed would work where technique already hadn't.

Dax absorbed the hit by turning with it, the same economy as always, and answered with an elbow Nadia barely managed to block with her forearm — the bone held, but the whole arm went numb at once, hanging useless for a second before she decided to keep fighting with the other one.

"DARA!" Oliver shouted. She was already at a dead run. Her eyes locked onto the target. Dax barely managed to turn — not fast enough.

"I KNOW!"

Dara came from behind — and not once. Two Doubles and Dara herself closed on Dax from three angles at once, the same trick that had worked in the Sant'Ambrogio parking garage — except Dax wasn't some ordinary candidate blinded by a flash of light. He read all three shapes in the same instant, discarded the two fakes on something none of them ever caught — a detail of posture, of weight, something a Double couldn't quite copy — and went straight for the real Dara. One single instant, the Odem in his feet doing all the work: a strike to the plexus landed before she could read the whole movement, and left her on her knees, gasping for air. One of the copies wrapped around Dax's back in a lock, the other around his legs, trying to bring him down. The real Dara's face strained against it, her consciousness barely holding on.

Pier tried once more what had already worked against Yegor — he weighted the ground under Dax's feet, the mass multiplying all at once. Dax didn't sink.

Oliver was the last to reach real range. Anchor first — Dax's body stopped fighting the pull for a second, the same genuine surprise crossing his face that Oliver had already seen once before. He charged both fists with Odem, one and then the other, both blows snapping Dax's head back like a punching bag. A line of blood welled up from his mouth, the smile still there.

Oliver's expression twisted, something like anger crossing his face. He drew back a third strike, but before he could land it, both of Dax's hands had already closed around his clothes.

"Wh—" He didn't get to finish the word before he understood: both copies were unraveling. Dara had lost consciousness. And by then it was already too late.

Dax's knee caught him in the stomach, driving out every bit of air, and for a second he was back in the fight against him in the pit. The hit landed hard enough that for a second he thought he was going to bring up what little he had in his stomach.

He staggered for a second, clumsy steps backward, Dax watching him from above with something like amusement.

"Give me more — yes! Keep it coming!" Dax said, almost giddy. It was the first time Oliver had seen him like this. And right then, he noticed something he hadn't caught before: Dax's eyes were full of an almost bloody glow, the canines in his mouth slightly longer than before.

And in that moment he felt his own body weigh ten times as much, driving him toward the ground. It was Pier.

"UP," Pier shouted at him, running past, touching his shoulder as he went. The weight eased, for a second.

---

The two men collided, arms leading, bone against bone wrapped in something more than muscle, both their weights multiplied several times over their real mass.

The first exchange came down to momentum alone — two weighted bodies meeting mid-charge, neither able to turn aside in time. The impact made a sound that no impact should make, and both of them fell back half a step, Pier with his whole arm numb, Dax with his jaw clenched.

Pier didn't give him time to fully catch his breath. He loaded his own fist with extra weight — the same principle he used against other people's footing, turned now on his own hand — and threw a short hook that Dax blocked with his forearm. The bone held, but the impact drove him back a full step, something no strike from Asha or Nadia had managed all night.

"That," Dax said, almost to himself, something like respect crossing his face, "just like that."

He didn't get time to say anything else. Riku came in from the flank, half his face swollen and caked in dirt, the push off the ground driving him straight into his side with a charged strike that dug into the point of contact just like the first one of the night. Oliver, still tasting bile in his mouth, closed off the other flank almost at the same time, his fists this time loaded with built-up Momentum.

The three of them struck almost together.

Dax took all three hits without going down, his whole body absorbing what no body should be able to absorb that many times in a row. A cut opened over his left eyebrow, blood running fast down that side of his face.

Dax answered the triple with a double.

Pier caught it first — a single blow to the chest loaded with something that was no longer just physical force. He flew back several meters, the weight he'd been holding on himself vanishing all at once as he lost consciousness mid-flight. Riku came next — Dax's leg aimed straight at his face mid-spin. He jumped, his feet loaded with Static.

Oliver caught the last one — the failed attack against Riku, reused. He held it off with both arms, the strain plain on his face.

Riku landed, catching himself against Dax's body, back to back with him, and Dax turned. A hook sailed over his head, wide enough for Riku to slip in underneath it. He threw one, then another, then another, each strike accelerated by Static — enough to push Dax back a step. Oliver took the opening, charged his fist, and released as much momentum as he could into Dax's side. The man's body gave, and for the first time that night, he was actually moved from where he stood.

The two of them stood facing each other for a moment, Riku's face bruised, Oliver's stance thrown off by pain.

"You look terrible," Riku said, something like a smirk in his voice.

"And your face is a mess," Oliver answered.

---

Yegor limped back on his own, his face tight with pain. Lukas arrived from the other end of the field almost at the same moment, breathing hard, the only one of his own group still standing — and from the way the two of them looked at each other for a second before moving, neither needed to say it out loud. Two sides that had spent the whole week without exchanging a word now landed on the same cold math at the same time: alone, neither of them was getting anywhere. Together, there was still something left to take from tonight.

They went straight for what was left of Valais, not for Dax.

Lukas was the first to find his prey. Pier, still on his knees from Dax's last hit, saw him coming from a distance. Bare fists, nothing charged yet. It didn't do him much good. Lukas absorbed the first hit with his shoulder and locked his arms around Pier before he could pull back — a full hold that drove the air out of his lungs at once.

Pier triggered Ballast on his own body, everything he had left, turning himself into dead weight multiplied tenfold in Lukas's arms — a real crack, tendon strained past what it was built to take, and Lukas's grip loosened a degree without fully letting go.

Riku was the first to notice, and moved before Oliver understood why. Nadia reacted the same way from a completely different angle.

They arrived together — Riku struck first, a sharp punch to Lukas's forearm where the tendon was already cracking, and Nadia drove her elbow into his side with all the weight she could gather from one good arm. Lukas took both without breaking his hold, jaw clenched, Pier's still-multiplied weight hanging off his arms like a sentence.

A few meters off, when Oliver turned, Yegor's feint was already halfway there. Oliver read it almost on reflex and braced against a cross that never actually came — Yegor followed up low with the other arm instead, a short shot to the ribs that drove the air out of him. Oliver didn't go down. He answered with an elbow, straight to the temple, and Yegor turned just enough that the blow split his eyebrow instead of putting him on the ground.

"Yegor," Oliver said, almost surprised.

"Me," Yegor answered, his expression bloodthirsty.

Riku went back to the same arm, the second punch landing right where the first had, and this time something actually gave — a different crack from before, louder, and Lukas screamed, his voice broken, almost guttural. Nadia used the scream to drive her elbow in again, higher this time, hunting for the shoulder, and Lukas, still not letting go of Pier, swung his whole body around to shove her off, sending her rolling across the grass.

Oliver took a second hit from Yegor in the same instant, the fist landing square on his cheekbone, and he tasted blood before he'd even decided to answer it — a short hook, charged with Odem, that sent Yegor stumbling back.

Riku, alone against Lukas now, loaded the last push he had left in him and threw himself at the same arm a third time, a strike charged with electricity aimed straight at the point where the tendon had already given once. This time Lukas's whole arm came apart, unable to hold anything, and Pier dropped to the ground between them — unconscious before he touched the grass, his whole body slack, with no one ever quite sure whether it was the hit, the lack of air, or simply that he had nothing left to stay conscious with.

Lukas stayed on his feet, the arm hanging wrong, breath ragged — but on his feet.

Yegor watched Oliver win the last exchange and didn't wait around to lose the next one. He threw what little Arsenal he had left against the ground between them — a short blast, enough to kick dirt and shredded grass into Oliver's face, not enough to hurt him — and used the second it bought him to shout Lukas's name and fall back toward Nadia instead of keeping up the fight.

Lukas was already turning that way before the shout had fully left Yegor's mouth. Riku, still catching his breath from the last hit to the broken arm, didn't make it in time to stop him — two steps, and he came up short, close enough to see it and too far to stop it.

---

Nadia saw both of them coming at once and didn't freeze. She blocked Yegor's first hit with her good forearm, the impact traveling up the bone whole, and spun to open distance before Lukas could close the other flank — but Lukas, down to one arm, only needed the one that still worked: he caught her by the shoulder, stopping her dead, just long enough for Yegor's fist to land with nothing left to stop it.

Nadia's good arm — the only whole one she had left — gave with the same sound Lukas's had a moment before, and she went down unable to raise either one. Both legs buckled with the pain.

Riku and Oliver converged on Lukas in the same instant, without needing to say so — Riku loading the last push he had left, Oliver releasing all the Momentum he'd built up through the whole exchange with Yegor into one concentrated strike.

Riku's hit landed first, straight to the already-broken arm. Lukas roared, but he didn't fall — planted his feet, the useless arm hanging, and threw the good one blind, pure instinct, no technique left in it. Riku barely dodged it. It was Oliver's fist that closed the account — all his Momentum released at once against the jaw. Lukas folded backward and didn't move again, his whole body slack before it touched the grass. The ground cracked a second later.

Yegor was alone now.

He turned toward the two of them, breath ragged, one arm hanging worse than the other, and still he didn't retreat — he read the distance between Riku and Oliver, the angle between them, and something in his expression shifted, calculating.

Oliver triggered Swap without warning, Riku's position and Yegor's trading places at once — he'd meant to land it behind him, but he'd miscalculated, half a step off, just enough for Yegor to turn in time to block. It didn't matter much. Oliver, the only one who knew in advance where Yegor would materialize, already had his fist charged and waiting at the exact spot — the strike landed clean on his side, a second before the Russian finished turning around.

Yegor stumbled back, and Oliver didn't give him time to recover his balance. He triggered Swap on himself — his position and Yegor's inverted in a blink — and reappeared with his fist already halfway there, the only one of the three who knew in advance exactly where he'd be standing. The blow sank into the man's ribs, and Riku closed in from the other flank in the same instant, a short jolt that tore a hoarse scream out of him.

Yegor didn't stay put. He read the pattern — two hits in a row, always right after a swap — and instead of waiting for the third, he loaded the last of his Arsenal into the ground under his own feet, a blast that launched him out of both their reach, gaining real distance for the first time in the whole fight.

It didn't do him much good. Oliver was already running at him, and halfway there he vanished again — Swap, his position for Yegor's once more — and reappeared already turned, his fist charged before the swap had even finished closing. Yegor took the hit full on, no time to read it this time, and the impact left him open just long enough for Riku to arrive from behind, his whole push off the ground released in one strike to the chest that lifted him off the ground.

Yegor dropped to his knees — but not all the way down.

And when Oliver triggered Swap once more to put Riku right on top of him and finish it up close, Yegor was already waiting for it. He'd read the pattern backward — not where Oliver would appear, but when the follow-up strike would land. His fist caught him mid-swap, in the exact instant Riku's body finished materializing and hadn't yet found its balance — a sharp blow, straight to the temple, and this time Riku actually went down, none of the flickers of trying to get back up that he'd shown every other time that night.

Oliver saw Riku fall and didn't stop to process it. He triggered Swap, his position for Yegor's once more — the man, still on his knees, didn't even have time to raise his good arm. The final strike closed his eyes before his body finished tipping sideways onto the grass — a punch to the jaw that shook his whole frame, already unconscious, against the ground.

Oliver stood there alone, his lungs burning, and looked around: Asha, Nadia, Dara, Pier, Riku, Lukas, Yegor — all of them down, in varying degrees of consciousness, none of them able to get up in time for anything anymore.

He was alone.
