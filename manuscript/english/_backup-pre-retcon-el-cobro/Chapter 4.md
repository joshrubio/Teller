# Chapter 4 — Registry

They hit the asphalt with a blow Oliver felt in his teeth.

Park absorbed the impact the same way he'd absorbed every one before it: redistributing, banking, wasting nothing. Oliver didn't. His body followed the jump's momentum straight into the ground, rolled over his left shoulder, and was back up before the pain finished registering. He kept running. Only when he caught his breath did he notice something burning in his right palm and the taste of metal on his tongue. He didn't stop to check it. But this time it took him a second longer than usual to get up, and that second was enough for Oliver to notice something had changed in his breathing.

"How much do you have left?" Oliver asked.

Park didn't answer right away. He got to his feet and swept the street with a quick glance, his eyes moving the same way Oliver had learned to move his own: looking for what was still, what was about to be.

"Enough," he said.

He didn't elaborate. But when they started moving again, he didn't pull Oliver along. He ran beside him.

It wasn't a small gesture. Through the whole chase Park had been the compass, the force, the one who set the trajectory and dragged Oliver when his legs hesitated. Now he ran at Oliver's pace, his eyes on the surroundings but his attention, Oliver noticed, on him.

On what he saw.

The fallen cables from the last block were still vibrating behind them, and as long as that tremor lasted the Domain couldn't stabilize there. But the front kept advancing. Oliver saw it in the filaments pulsing north, closing over blocks that hadn't given way yet, and knew, with a certainty that didn't have a name yet, that the margin was shrinking with every second.

"There," he said, pointing left without stopping. "Don't step there."

Park changed course mid-stride. The spot Oliver had pointed to caved inward an instant later, the concrete torn downward with that dry blow they'd both learned to recognize in their chests before they heard it.

They kept running.

---

Park didn't move when he felt the pressure return. For the first time since they'd escaped the building, he stopped looking for an escape route. His feet stayed planted on the cracked asphalt while the cables around them lost tension, one by one, until they went still.

Oliver noticed immediately.

"Park... what are you doing?"

There was no immediate answer. The air around him began to vibrate in a way different from before — not like an external tremor, but as if the space itself were charging. Every small movement he'd made during the chase — every jump, every sharp change of direction, every impact absorbed against walls and ground — seemed to be condensing now into his posture, into the rigidity of his shoulders, into the way his hands slowly closed.

It wasn't nervous tension.

It was accumulation.

"I can't keep dodging," he said finally, with a strained calm. "I get how it works now."

Oliver felt a chill.

The lines began appearing again, this time directly in front of them, converging on a point in the air where nothing was visible. The space there darkened, not like a shadow, but like an absence of surface, a void that reflected neither light nor depth.

Then it appeared, right in front of them. It didn't move. It didn't materialize. It was simply there. Oliver sucked in a breath, choking back a shout.

An incomplete shape, almost human in proportion, but without defined features. Its body seemed made of condensed pressure, of invisible tensions holding a barely stable silhouette. Where a face should have been there was only a dark concavity, a hollow that seemed to absorb the gaze rather than return it.

And still, Oliver knew with absolute certainty that it was looking at them.

The air turned heavy.

The unbinding lines began pulling taut from the ground, the cables, the nearby buildings, all of it pointing toward that empty center.

Park stepped forward.

The asphalt beneath his foot fractured with a dry sound.

"It doesn't erase out of hatred," he said quietly, as if forcing himself to think out loud. "It erases for balance. What stops resisting... it lets go."

Oliver didn't understand why he was talking.

Until he saw it.

Park's body wasn't actually still. To the eye it looked motionless, but small vibrations ran through his muscles, his clothes, even the air around him. It was as if thousands of micro-movements were stacking on a single point, generating a contained pressure.

He was staying in motion — without moving.

"So..." Park went on, fixing his gaze on the dark shape. "The only thing it can't erase... is something that keeps pushing back."

The ground under their feet groaned.

Oliver felt the change before he saw it. A brutal sense of weight compressed into the air, as if gravity had increased only in that space. Loose fragments of asphalt began to vibrate, unable to stay still.

The Authority reacted at once.

The lines pulled taut toward Park, faster than before, converging on his chest like invisible jaws ready to close.

Oliver shouted.

"Park!"

But this time Park didn't back away.

He exhaled slowly.

And released the inertia.

It wasn't a visible movement, or a strike in any conventional sense. It was a brutal discharge of acceleration built up through the entire chase, released in a single instant. The air exploded with a deep sound, as if something enormous had been compressed and let go all at once.

The invisible pressure struck the point where the Authority manifested. For a fraction of a second, the dark silhouette deformed, as if its shape were made of tensions that could shift.

It wasn't destroyed.

But it recoiled.

Oliver saw it clearly: the lines converging toward that center loosened, vibrating chaotically, unable to hold their perfect alignment.

For the first time since they'd entered the Domain, the phenomenon had been interrupted.

Park staggered, his body shaking violently from the effort. He was breathing hard, as if he had emptied something far deeper than his physical strength.

"I can't... hold this much longer..." he murmured.

Oliver didn't take his eyes off the Authority, because now he saw it better — not just its shape, but the lines that held it up.

Thousands of invisible filaments connecting it to every still surface in the Domain, anchoring it to the world like roots.

And at the center of all of them:

There was a point.

A single point where all of them crossed.

Something inside Oliver's perception clicked into place — as if he'd always known to look there.

"Park..." he said, his voice shaking. "You just need to... break the right connection."

He got up slowly, eyes fixed on that invisible center.

Park didn't answer. His knees were still sunk in the cracked asphalt. His muscles shook from the effort. His breathing came out uneven, as if each inhale had to force its way into his chest. Even so, he didn't look away from the point Oliver was pointing to — that invisible center where the unbinding lines converged like taut veins holding an open wound in the world. He couldn't see them, but he could feel the tension. Whatever Oliver could see, he could feel.

"Break the connection..." he repeated quietly, more to himself than to Oliver. "That means I can still reach it."

The Authority reacted before he finished the sentence. The lines pulled taut all at once, thousands of invisible threads closing toward them in a movement that no longer looked like a single attack but like a giant jaw trying to bite down on the entire space where they stood. The ground groaned under the pressure, the nearby facades vibrated, and the air compressed with a dry sound that made their ears ache.

Park got to his feet in the same instant.

He didn't retreat.

He planted his right foot and the asphalt gave under him, not from impact, but from accumulated pressure. His body began vibrating again with that imperceptible movement, the stored inertia pulsing in every muscle like an engine about to break.

"Then I won't dodge anymore," he said, staring at the dark silhouette. "If everything converges... so will I."

And he advanced.

He didn't run in a straight line. His body moved with impossible changes of direction, zigzagging between invisible trajectories Oliver could barely follow with his eyes. Every step released small bursts of acceleration that tore up the ground beneath his feet, launching him forward again as if the world itself were throwing him.

The Authority's bites descended on him from every side, tearing chunks out of the concrete, bending metal poles, ripping through the shadows of the buildings as if they were solid matter. A fragment flew off at a diagonal and grazed Oliver's right cheekbone with a dry edge. It wasn't a blow — it was a graze, the kind that takes two seconds to burn and a third to bleed. Oliver put his hand to his face on instinct, pulled it back with a dark line on his fingers, and looked forward again.

Park moved through that chaos without ever stopping, keeping his body in constant acceleration, refusing to stay in one place long enough to be thrown out of it.

Oliver watched him advance and understood what he was doing. It wasn't a retreat. It was a charge.

Every impact he avoided, every jump, every turn, every violent stop — it all accumulated. The inertia wasn't dissipating; it was being stored inside him as a growing reserve of force.

The air began trembling around him.

"He's charging..." Oliver murmured, feeling the pressure rise even several meters away.

The Authority sensed it too.

Its shape contracted, the unbinding lines pulling even tighter toward the central point, as if trying to reinforce its own stability before the impact. The space around it turned dense, heavy, hard to move through.

Park didn't slow down.

He took one last step, sinking his foot halfway into the asphalt, and his body moved with such speed that it vanished from its place. And for an instant, Oliver could see him again. Suspended in the air behind the Authority. Mid-motion.

All the inertia gathered through the entire chase compressed into that instant.

His leg came down violently, blurring in the process.

He exhaled.

"Die, you son of a bitch—"

And he struck.

A brutal discharge of acceleration released in a single instant, spreading out from his body like an explosion that deformed the air and crushed the surroundings. The ground fractured in a radius of several meters, the nearby glass shattered, and the Authority's own silhouette twisted violently, as if its internal tensions had been forced in incompatible directions.

For the first time, its shape broke.

It didn't disintegrate, but its outline scattered into fragments of unstable pressure that vibrated without aligning correctly. The lines holding it up loosened, trembling like cables under overload.

Park fell forward, catching himself with one hand on the ground, his body spent, his breath ragged.

"That's it..." he murmured, his voice barely audible. "Should be..."

But Oliver didn't feel relief — he could see it, the lines hadn't disappeared. They were still there, trembling. And then they began reordering themselves.

The dark silhouette began compressing again, slowly, rebuilding its shape out of those invisible connections, as if every still surface in the Domain were pulling on it to bring it back to stability.

Oliver understood in that instant. Park had struck its shape, but not its anchor.

"Park, it's not over!" he shouted, stepping forward without realizing it.

Park raised his eyes, exhausted, just in time to see the lines pull taut toward him again. But this time there wasn't enough stored energy to escape.

Oliver ran, on instinct, without knowing exactly why.

And the Authority lunged.

The bite descended from the air, invisible, inevitable, closing on the space where Park stood, tearing up the ground around him in a perfect circle.

Only a couple of meters away, Oliver's eyes fixed on the web of lines holding up the entity, and for the first time he didn't see them just as pathways, but as variables.

Every line had a purpose, a moment when something had stopped resisting, an exact point where it had been unbound. And in the middle of all of them — the origin.

"You don't need to break it..." he said, his voice steady despite the shaking in his body. "You just need it to stop existing."

Then the air began pulling taut around him, almost exactly the way it did around the Authority. But more compressed, more static, faster.

The air compressed around Oliver as if an invisible pressure had descended over him. It wasn't a sudden burst or a violent flash, but a growing tension, almost physical, that spread from his eyes into the space around him. The unbinding lines, those invisible threads holding the Authority's shape, began vibrating in response, recognizing him in a way he himself didn't understand.

Park saw it.

And for the first time since he'd known him, he didn't see a confused intern or a kid out of place, but someone touching something that shouldn't exist in human hands.

"What... the hell...?" he managed, still on his knees, his voice broken with effort.

Oliver didn't answer.

His eyes were locked on the core of the invisible tangle, where all the lines converged into a single point of tension. His breathing turned uneven, and a faint tremor ran through his hands, as if he were holding a weight no one else could see.

The space reacted.

The lines didn't just pull taut: they began dragging toward him, as if his presence were altering their natural orientation. The air around his body vibrated with a low, constant sound, like the hum of a transformer about to fail.

Then he stepped forward, and dropped his full weight onto the Authority's core, where the lines converged, with a single motion. His fist.

The Authority thrashed violently.

Its dark shape contracted and expanded at once, as if trying to reorganize its structure against an unexpected interference. The bites that had been closing in on Park stopped halfway, suspended in an impossible tension.

Oliver clenched his teeth and drove his hand in, cutting through the tension the Authority was made of.

The lines responded.

Spreading over the Authority like cracking ice, exactly as he had seen before, breaking everything apart.

The ground shook under his feet.

The Authority made no sound — it had no mouth to make one; instead it let out a screech like thousands of circuits burning out at once. The whole environment vibrated with an unbearable pressure, as if the Domain itself were protesting what was happening.

And then Oliver did the only thing his instinct told him to.

He pulled.

It wasn't graceful, it was clumsy, desperate, physical. As if he were trying to tear something rooted deep in the world out by its base.

The lines stretched to an impossible limit and, one by one, began coming loose from the fabric of the Domain, disconnecting from the environment that held them. The Authority's shape collapsed in the process, its silhouette shrinking to an ever-smaller core, unable to hold its coherence without those bonds.

For an instant, it hung suspended in the air, a mass of dark tension, vibrating on the edge of vanishing.

Oliver gave one last pull and the lines let go completely.

The Authority didn't explode, didn't dissolve. It simply stopped being there. Slowly, like a file deleted without a trace.

And against Oliver's chest, in the inside pocket of his jacket, the Ledger went cold all at once — the mild warmth it had carried all night switching off like an unplugged heater. For the first time since the basement, it weighed exactly what plastic weighs.

The silence that followed was absolute.

And then the Domain began to collapse.

The grayish sky fractured into overlapping planes, the empty streets warped like reflections in disturbed water, and the buildings lost their consistency, fading into fragments of light and shadow that scattered in every direction.

Park felt the change before he saw it.

Weight returned to his body all at once, normal gravity settling like a slab after the unnatural absence of the Domain. The air felt dense again, cold, real.

And Oliver fell.

His body dropped forward as if the strings holding it up had been cut. Park reacted on pure instinct, but he was a fraction of a second late. Oliver hit the asphalt face-first. Park reached him immediately after, grabbed him by the shoulder, and turned him over.

The kid was shaking violently.

His breathing was shallow, broken, and a thin line of blood ran from one nostril. His eyes were open, but unfocused, unable to fix on anything solid.

"Hey..." Park murmured, shaking him gently. "Hey, look at me."

Oliver blinked with difficulty.

"I think..." he said, in a thread of a voice, "that... that shouldn't have been so easy."

Before Park could answer, the world came back all at once.

The noise arrived first.

A deep, dry blast that cut through the air like a whip, followed by a chain of distant explosions echoing across the city. The lights that had begun to stabilize in nearby buildings flickered violently and went out all at once.

Grayhaven fell into darkness.

From where he stood, on the street where they had landed, Park watched the blackout spread in waves across the financial district. Entire towers went dark, billboard screens shut off simultaneously, and the constant hum of the electrical infrastructure disappeared as if someone had torn the energy heart out of the city.

The resulting silence was unnatural, heavy, real.

Park raised his eyes to the dark horizon, understanding the scale of what had just happened.

"Great..." he murmured, exhaling with exhaustion. "You just killed an Authority — and knocked out power to half the city."

He looked down at Oliver, who was barely holding on to consciousness in his arms.

"And now..." he added quietly, "the Agency is going to want a lot of explanations."
