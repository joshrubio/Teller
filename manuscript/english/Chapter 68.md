# Chapter 68 — Pattern

The date arrived on Monday, not as a confirmed mission but as a parameter: tentative evaluation date, Authority classification pending confirmation, team to be determined. Voss called the proto-team into the office in the north wing. Oliver, Riku, Dara, Pier, Asha. A date on the calendar and four walls.

Nobody asked questions. Everyone left knowing the same thing and thinking different things.

Oliver spent the next two weeks in a way the word training didn't quite cover. Mornings with Maren: pushing the selective-attention threshold past the point of controlled saturation, learning exactly where the edge sat so he could work close to it without crossing it. Afternoons in the gym: Tension, Anchor, Impulse — activating and holding until the cost stopped surprising him. Nights on the floor with his eyes closed, redistributing Odem from one point of the body to another, trying to hold two positions at once without the second consuming the first.

The day before tactical training he spent without opening the notebook once. There was nothing left to write down.

---

The summons arrived Thursday morning: five names, a time, work clothes.

Oliver reached the outdoor space in the south wing and found the other four already there. Dara stretching with the focus of someone who turns a warm-up into real preparation. Pier standing with his arms crossed, reading the space with eleven years of looking at training rooms. Riku adjusting his left wristband, tighter than the right, always. Asha leaning against the back wall, still.

Park stood in the center.

"Three minutes, no Domain," he said. "After that, open. The goal isn't to beat me. The goal is to keep functioning as a unit. When you stop doing that, we stop."

Maren stood off to the side with the notebook on her knee.

Nobody asked about the "if."

---

The first three minutes made something clear that none of them had said out loud: Park wasn't training. He was evaluating.

Dara got there first. She drove her elbow into Park's side before anyone else had closed the circle — a real hit, well placed. Park rotated with the impact instead of against it, absorbed Dara's momentum, and gave it back: a forearm shove to the shoulder that sent her two meters back and left her recalibrating. It wasn't a block. It was a response.

Pier came second. He came in low, trying to destabilize the center of gravity. Park dropped too, hooked Pier's leg with his knee, and used Pier's own weight to turn him — Pier ended up with his back to Park for the second it took him to reorient, and that second was enough for Park to close the radius against Asha, who was coming in from the flank.

Asha tried a grip on the shoulder. Park didn't block it — he let it land, turned his arm inward, and by the time Asha followed the movement, Park was no longer where the grip expected him to be. Asha ended up carried a step past where she'd planned by her own momentum.

Riku arrived last and from further out — he knew close contact wasn't his best position. Park moved toward him before Riku had picked his angle, drove a forearm into his chest with enough weight to move him half a meter, and left him outside the radius without further ceremony.

Oliver didn't go in head-on. He read from the margin. Park's Domain accumulating with no outlet.

"Park's going center-right. Asha, close the side."

Asha closed with open hands — looking for the neck or the shoulder, any sustained point of contact where she could apply resistance and slow Park's next move. Riku had shifted to the back in the meantime, calculating the flanking angle that would leave Park with the fewest exits — the position from which, when the moment came to release Static, the radius would land on Park without reaching any of his own.

Park went for the space Asha had opened by closing in. He passed between them before either finished arriving: he went for Riku first, drove a forearm into his side while Riku was still measuring the angle, and turned him with his own momentum to put him outside the radius. Riku landed on the side mat shoulder-first, got up, returned to position.

"Again," Asha said, and the group went again.

This time Pier went first. Not head-on — he took the oblique angle where Park's weight would be least distributed, waited for the step, and at the exact moment Park shifted his center of gravity to his left foot, drove a palm into his right side. It wasn't brute force: it was geometry applied to another person's body, the kind of intervention someone with eleven years of reading mass and balance executes when he doesn't have his Domain available. Park felt it late — the point of application was right and the timing was right. He absorbed the push with a rotated hip, redistributed it, and gave it back in the same direction: Pier ended up two meters from where he'd started, carried there by his own momentum.

Dara reached Park from behind with a forearm to the neck, a clean grip. Park lowered his center of gravity, bent his knees, and turned — Dara flew over his shoulder and hit the ground with a dull thud that echoed through the whole space. She got up immediately. Her teeth were clenched.

Asha came in from the side and got a hand on Park's shoulder, trying to anchor the point of contact. Park turned his arm inward, broke the grip, and returned the movement — Asha ended up with her own arm twisted behind her back for the second it took her to break free.

Oliver called positions, the group responded, Park adjusted. The three minutes ended with the group breathing hard and Park exactly where he'd started.

Pier had the posture of an active Domain charge. Riku's left wristband had loosened a finger's width. Asha was rolling her shoulder slowly.

Everyone was ready. So was Park, though nobody could have said how it showed.

Oliver stepped away from the side.

He concentrated Odem in his forearms — the redistribution he'd spent weeks practicing on the floor of his room, the same exercise he'd read in Park and in Yusuf. Anchor at the center, extend to the point of contact before impact.

Park saw him coming. He didn't change his stance — he waited. Oliver reached the contact radius and tried to grip Park's forearm. The difference was that he had Odem charged at the point of contact before their hands crossed.

Park felt it — the same micro-pause as when Oliver had activated Tension, but from a different source. This wasn't a copy. It was Oliver's own body doing something Park recognized.

Even so, Park broke the grip.

He did it more slowly than he'd broken Asha's. Oliver held on for two seconds before Park turned the axis and shifted him outward — not a throw, just repositioning. Oliver landed on his feet, not the mat.

Park looked at him for a moment. He didn't say anything.

Park's signature changed.

Oliver saw the signature change before they felt it in their bodies, and said:

"Now."

Pier went first. Gravity on the right side of the space increased — a dead zone that tripled the effort of every step there. Park calculated the edge of the field and started working outside it, but Pier had been waiting for exactly that: the weight landed exactly where Park had calculated it wouldn't. Park stopped for half a second — real, visible — and in that half second Dara went for the left flank and Asha went for the center.

Dara arrived. She drove a forearm into Park's side, a clean hit, and Park absorbed it with a rotated hip — the energy of the impact going sideways instead of into the center. Park counterattacked with an elbow to Dara's shoulder before she finished the movement: Dara went two meters backward and needed the wall to keep from falling.

"The Double," Oliver said. "Now, from the other side."

Dara deployed the Double without fully recovering her position: the projection materialized three meters to Park's left, identical in weight and posture. Park looked at both figures for less than a second — left foot forward on the real one, as Oliver had seen — and instead of attacking Dara, went for the Double with enough force to make her choose: hold the projection or dodge. Dara dodged. The Double dissolved. Park used the moment Dara redirected her attention to close inside her radius and shove her with his shoulder — Dara hit the ground again, this time without the wall.

"Don't put it away," Oliver said. "Hold two."

Riku released Static. The lights along the side flickered and went out. The clock lost its digits. The room went into real dimness — enough light from the courtyard outside to make out silhouettes, not angles. Riku was the only one who operated well under those conditions: the left-side asymmetry gave him location without sight. Park didn't need Odem to move either.

Oliver tried something he hadn't planned.

Asha's Tension — conditions 0–3, weeks of practice — activated at Park's ankle. Not immobilizing: the kind of friction that interrupts a sequence without stopping it outright. The cost hit immediately under multiple loads.

Park felt something. Half a step recalibrated, weight shifted to the opposite axis. And then Park went straight for Oliver.

Oliver didn't fully manage to dodge — Park caught his forearm, redirected the momentum, and Oliver ended up against the side wall, his shoulder hitting the concrete before he broke free. It wasn't a hard impact — it was enough to make clear Park knew exactly where the Tension had come from.

"Different signature," Asha said, without looking at Oliver.

The group had Park boxed in for the first time: Pier's gravitational field on the left, Riku's Static covering the flank, Dara with two simultaneous Doubles from different angles — Oliver could see in her face how much it cost her to hold them — and Asha closing the center. Park's range of movement was cut in half. Oliver was coordinating from the side with his shoulder aching, losing the finer margins but still reading the middle layers.

He read Park adjust his axis — he was about to exit through Riku's side. A second before he moved, Oliver activated Anchor.

Not on Park directly: on the ground under his left foot, the point of contact, fixing it to the substrate for half a second. The cost landed on top of the Tension cost still lingering — double load, simultaneous reading of five active signatures, the selective-attention threshold grazing the edge. But Anchor answered.

Park felt it. His left foot didn't lift when it should have. Half a second of stillness he wasn't expecting.

Pier was already on the correct axis. The gravitational field dropped right on top of Park in that half second, and he drove his shoulder into Park's center of gravity with enough weight for a clean impact. Park absorbed it — the hip rotated, the energy distributed — but the blow moved him a full step backward.

It was the only clean hit the group landed on him.

Park looked at Pier. Then he looked at Oliver. He didn't say anything.

Then Park escalated.

Oliver read the signature charging and shouted:

"Dara, out of the center."

Dara didn't have time to process the instruction before Park moved — but Oliver had already activated Impulse, a straight line from the side, pushing Dara half a meter to the right. It wasn't enough to get her fully clear. Stage 2's impact caught her at an angle instead of head-on: Dara spun on her axis and went down with her arm cushioning the fall instead of taking the full weight in the chest.

Asha didn't have that cover. She reached the point of contact and the released momentum hit her shoulder with a force that didn't match the visible movement: it threw her two meters back and left her bracing a hand against the mat. Pier caught the rebound in his own gravitational field and lost his footing.

Stage 2. Accumulation released from rest. No run-up, no signal, no visible preparation.

Anchor. Impulse. Tension before that. Three copies under simultaneous load on top of the active reading of five signatures. Oliver felt the threshold cross before he could do anything about it.

Minute eight.

The reading degraded at the edges and then at the center. Volume and position — no internal structure.

"Pier's going right."

Pier had already gone left. Park passed between Asha and the space where Dara's Double had been.

Pier took control in two seconds.

But the momentum was broken. The next ninety seconds were accumulated deterioration: Riku extended Static past the safe radius and accidentally cut Asha's line of sight. Dara and Pier covered the same zone. Oliver was still on his feet but seeing only outlines — mass, speed, no internal structure.

Park stopped.

Still in the center. The group stopped. Asha was gripping her shoulder with her free hand. Dara had a knee on the ground, not from falling but because it was easier than standing. Riku had adjusted his wristband again. Pier breathed slowly through his nose.

Park had a red mark on his chest where Pier had hit him. That was all.

He looked at Pier.

"Two seconds."

Pier nodded.

That was it.

---

Oliver stayed a moment in the space while the others packed up. Asha stopped beside him before leaving. She was still gripping her shoulder.

"The Tension was you," she said. Not a question. "And the other thing. The ground, when Park was about to exit through Riku."

"Anchor." A pause. "And Impulse when Park escalated. To get you out of the center."

Asha looked at him for a moment.

"You didn't get me out of the center."

"I got you out of the direct hit."

Asha didn't answer right away. She picked up her water bottle.

"Next time, warn me first," she said, and left.

Oliver thought about Sisi.

Not because she'd missed training — plenty of subjects weren't there. But because he'd spent weeks reviewing the operational-evaluation briefings and her name hadn't appeared in any of them. Her sessions with Maren were still marked as active evaluation, not operational preparation. The others — Riku, Dara, even Pier, who hadn't had an external activation in eleven years — had visible progress toward field parameters. Sisi didn't. And Oliver didn't know whether it was the Domain, her history of distrust toward AMAT, or something nobody had told him yet.

The asymmetry was there.

---

The conversation between Park and Voss took place in the north-wing office half an hour after training. Voss had two folders open on the desk when Park came in. He didn't close them.

Park sat down.

"The February transport," Voss said, gesturing to the one on the left. "Geneva initiated the extraction protocol without prior consultation. The escort agent never reached the delivery point. The catalogued entity wasn't recovered." A pause. "Three events with no documented connection between them."

Park looked at the folder on the right. He didn't ask what was in it.

"East ridge of the perimeter," he said. "Ten days ago, with Yusuf. Snow disturbed between two pines. A branch bent from above. A week old, maybe more. Direct line of sight to the main building."

Voss didn't write anything down. He listened.

"Same vector," Voss said. "The pattern isn't aimed at the building. It's aimed at what's inside it." A pause. "Someone who's spent enough time in the field to leave no more trace than what you two found."

"A survival Domain," Park said.

"Or forty years of practice," Voss said. "Probably both."

Park waited.

"Something that should have ended forty years ago," Voss said, quietly. Not as an explanation. As a thought that had been held back and found, here, in this office, its first release.

"Do you know who it is?"

"I have a name."

Park waited again.

"I'd rather you didn't have it yet," Voss said.

---

Oliver found out about the conversation the only way he found out about almost anything at the Center: he crossed paths with Park in the north-wing corridor an hour later and read the signature without deciding to. Not altered. Quieter — the specific way things go quiet after receiving a confirmation they'd been waiting for without knowing they were waiting for it.

He didn't ask.

Back in his room, the Strength signature was still there in the background. Weak, without active structure, but present. Sometime in the coming weeks he'd need to learn to release it in a controlled way. Then use it on something that wasn't an exercise.

Eight minutes. The target was twelve.

It wasn't fear he felt. It was the specific sensation of being exactly where he was supposed to be and not yet knowing whether that was enough — and, quieter than that, the awareness that the answer had a date he didn't get to choose.
