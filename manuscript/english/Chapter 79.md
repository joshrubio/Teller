# Chapter 79 — The Assignment

---

Voss had called him that same morning, barely a few hours after the last vehicle left the Valais Center. No ceremony, no long explanation — just confirmation that Lev and Sabine hadn't turned up at any checkpoint, on any list of relocated personnel, or anywhere else they should have turned up if they'd followed the exit protocol like everyone else.

*Fresh trail, Park. Fresher than any lead we're going to have after today.*

She hadn't mentioned St. Gotthard, or Valais, or any other place Park might have expected to end up. Just the name of a city and the hour by which he needed to have a team ready. Park hadn't asked why him — the answer would have been the same one Voss had already given him before, in another life, on another mission, and he didn't need to hear it twice to know it still weighed the same.

The team was already waiting for him on the flight: three names Voss had sent him in writing, with no more context than rank and specialty. Elena Krauss, Rank 2, Trail Domain — she read the residual disturbance a body with a notable reserve of Odem left in ambient density as it passed, an echo that faded with the hours until it disappeared entirely. Marc Delacroix and Sofia Reyes, both Rank 3, neither with a Domain — the kind of agent AMAT produced in greater numbers than any other type, trained until discipline did the work others did with Odem. Park had spent less than a day working with the three of them, and had already learned to tell their silences apart: Marc's was contained impatience; Sofia's, calculation; Elena's, something harder to name, something Park recognized without being able to explain where the recognition came from.

The full file on Lev and Sabine had arrived with them, and Park had read it twice during the flight — Lev's Heat Domain, cataloged as high Level 2 with "undocumented advanced use" flagged in red, no further detail; Sabine's Anchor, Level 1, considered stable and well integrated until the day it stopped being so. Nothing in the file explained why they'd left. AMAT rarely included that kind of context in operational documents — the question of "why" wasn't relevant to the mission of locating them, only the "where" and the "how" were. Park closed the tablet before finishing the last page. He didn't need the rest to know that at some point in the next few hours he was going to have to decide how much of that "why" mattered to him, even if it didn't matter to the file.

He thought, briefly, of a street in Grayhaven, downed cables and buildings giving way around him, barely a few months back, and of an order he hadn't fully carried out the way he'd been expected to. He didn't finish the thought. It wasn't the time yet.

Nobody had ever asked him whether he'd make the same decision again under the same circumstances. Park hadn't asked himself out loud either, not even in his own head, until Voss called him this morning with a name and a city and no instructions about what to do if the outcome looked too much like something he'd already lived through once. With Lev and Sabine there was no civilian caught in the middle, no life hanging on a decision made in seconds. But a similar choice was waiting somewhere in the next few hours all the same: carry out the order exactly as written, or carry it out in a way he could keep defending afterward, even if nobody else understood it the same way.

The trail still smelled fresh when Park reached the safe house — literally, in the simplest sense: coffee made less than six hours ago, still with a trace of residual heat in the pot when Sofia Reyes set her palm above it without touching the metal directly.

"They left fast," she said. "But not out of panic. There's order in how they left this."

Park walked the apartment once more, slowly, cataloging what Sofia and Marc had already marked with colored tape on the floor — entry points, exit, a second exit point nobody had used yet but that someone had left prepared, just in case. Less than twenty-four hours since the Incursion, and Lev and Sabine had already had time to find this place, equip it, and abandon it again.

"Someone's helping them," Park said.

"Or they've spent months preparing an exit they didn't end up needing until now," Elena Krauss said, from the doorway, not fully entering. "Lev didn't trust anyone inside AMAT. It wouldn't be strange if he'd set this up alone, long before deciding to use it."

Park didn't rule out either possibility. He'd spent less than a day working with this team — Elena, Marc, Sofia, all three from the Lyon branch, none with more than two shared missions with him before this one — and he'd already learned enough to know that Elena preferred the explanation that didn't require a traitor inside the institution, and that she preferred it for a reason she hadn't explained to anyone yet.

"What do you think?" Park asked her directly, because Elena had stayed quiet longer than anyone with a stake in the discussion should have.

Elena stepped away from the doorway just enough to come all the way in, looking at the second exit point marked on the floor before answering.

"I think whoever set this up knew exactly what an AMAT team would look for when it came in," she said. "The exit points are where we'd look for them, not where someone improvising would put them. That's not Lev's paranoia. That's someone who knows the manual."

"Someone on the inside?" Marc said, looking up this time.

"I didn't say that," Elena said. "I said they know the manual. There are more people with access to that manual than AMAT would like to admit."

Park filed the answer away without commenting on it. It was the kind of observation that at another time would have earned a full note in the report, and that today was just one more piece among too many loose pieces.

He walked through the rest of the apartment while Marc and Sofia finished marking the perimeter. The main bedroom had the bed made, with a precision that didn't fit an emergency departure — someone had taken the time to leave everything in order, or someone had wanted it to look that way. In the closet, enough clothes for two people still hung, nothing especially expensive or especially cheap, the kind of wardrobe chosen not to draw attention in any particular setting. On the nightstand, a glass of water half drunk, no dust on the surface yet.

Park touched the glass without lifting it. Cold, but not cold enough to have been sitting there more than a few hours.

"This isn't an exit planned months in advance," he said, out loud, more to himself than to the team. "This is people who left fast but knew exactly where to go when they did. Both things at once."

"Which tells us someone was waiting for them here," Elena said, from the bedroom doorway, "or they had this place ready before any of this started. I keep coming back to the same thing. I don't like either version."

"Neither do I," Park said. "But only one of the two tells us something about who else knows exactly where we're standing right now."

"How much of a head start do they have on us?" Park said.

"Twelve hours, if they left the moment they heard about the Incursion," Marc said, not looking up from the phone he'd been comparing against a map of the area. "Less, if they waited to see if anyone came looking for them first."

"Park," Sofia said, from the window. "You should see this."

He went over. Sofia was pointing at a spot on the street, two floors down — a car double-parked, engine off, no one visible inside, but the windows clean enough for Park to make out, on the back seat, something that shouldn't be there: a folded blanket, the kind used for sleeping on long drives, still holding the shape of a body pressed into the fabric.

"That car wasn't there when we arrived," Marc said, already on his feet, already moving toward the door without anyone asking him to.

Park felt the exact weight of the moment when a lead stops being a cold trail and becomes something still breathing nearby. He said nothing. He made a gesture with two fingers — down, now — and Elena had already knelt by the doorframe, palm extended without quite touching the wood, reading something only she could feel.

"Someone went out this way less than an hour ago," she said, low. "The trace is still marked. But it's weak. Weaker than it should be at this distance."

"Weak how?" Park said.

"Like someone smoothed it out on purpose," Elena said, already back on her feet. "I don't know how to do that myself. I didn't know anyone could."

They went down the service stairs, not the elevator, Sofia first, Marc closing the line with the same economy of movement Park had seen in agents with twice his experience. On the second-floor landing, Elena ran her hand along the railing without fully stopping, and the gesture — almost automatic, something she probably did without thinking in any building she crossed — told her what she needed to know before anyone asked: no one else had come down that way in the last few hours.

At the entrance door, Park stopped just long enough to look once more at the car through the frosted glass. The blanket was still there. No shadow had moved inside since Sofia pointed it out.

"Sofia, cover the north exit," Park said, low. "Marc, with me. Elena, stay at the threshold until I say."

Nobody asked why. They went out.

The street air carried the particular cold of a city that still hadn't decided whether that morning was going to bring rain, and Park crossed the first stretch of sidewalk with his eyes fixed on the car, calculating angles, calculating what part of his body would be exposed if whatever was inside that vehicle decided the blanket wasn't needed anymore. He was ten steps away when the driver's door opened from the inside, slowly, no apparent hurry, and a hand — not Lev's, not Sabine's, a hand Park didn't recognize from any file he'd read that morning — stayed visible for a moment on the edge of the window before the rest of the person began to climb out of the car.
