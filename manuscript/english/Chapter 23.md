# Chapter 23 — Kiki

What was in the clearing wasn't one.

Park saw it before he reached the edge: eight shapes spread across the twenty meters of open space, all with the sector's high legibility — they were gwisin, the report described them as Level 3 Authorities with various capabilities. Vengeful spirits. Around them the Odem created a field that, on contact, caused disorientation and paralysis. Park had heard stories about them since he was a boy. He advanced two steps and scanned the zone with his eyes. Two agents on the ground, one moving, the other still. The remaining three of the advance team held the center with what they had.

The most urgent problem: the containment agent in the center-north, no cover, with two gwisin closing in from the northwest and the east at once. If they arrived at the same time, the agent couldn't handle both.

Park went in and headed northwest first.

The gwisin came straight on — the shape of an adult man, the Odem it gave off distorting the air like a kind of haze. Park planted his left foot, took the field on his right forearm, stored it. Momentum. Turned on his left foot. Released. The gwisin went into the tree at the south edge — bark torn away, edges less precise. Not dissolved, but off-line. Park felt his vision blur a second later. He closed his eyes and focused on his Odem. Pressed. Until the Odem covered him like a second skin. That would be enough.

He turned his eyes back to the clearing; that had left the one to the east alone. The containment agent got the angle.

The next problem: the cover agent at the east edge was giving ground. Three more meters and the line broke. Park went east — four steps, the inertia of the first exchange still in him, the steps faster than his legs alone could explain. He reached the gwisin's flank and instead of taking the impact head-on turned his body ninety degrees on the last step, letting the field pass grazing his side, and from there threw a rising elbow at the point where the figure was densest. The gwisin bent backward with the impact and Park finished the turn by pushing with an open palm — sent it into the center of the clearing where the containment agent was repositioning. Two resolved with the same unit.

Two resolved, six left from the initial count. The perimeter delivered three more in thirty seconds — nine active now.

One of the new ones reached the agent carrying a modified weapon before Park could intercept it — the gwisin passed through the agent's field and the agent lost his footing, went down on his knees, weapon on the ground. The same gwisin oriented toward the containment agent, four meters off with no cover. One of the new arrivals pressed from the north on the third agent. Park had two options: the one from the north, or the one heading for the containment agent. The one from the north would arrive in three seconds. The one heading for the containment agent in two.

He went for the containment agent.

A meter from the agent he cut the gwisin's path with a low kick to the figure's ankle — not to knock it down, to break its arrival timing. The gwisin lost half a second of sync, and Park used that half second to slide his left forearm under the attack line, absorbing the full impact without redirecting it, just blocking. The Class-R on his arm activated to maximum from the support surge. With the gwisin destabilized, Park closed his right fist and struck the core of the field — sent it to the ground. The earth gave, root exposed.

The gwisin from the north reached the third agent. The agent gave two meters before recovering position — the gwisin fell back to the edge of the clearing, wounded in shape but active, and added itself to the general pressure on the perimeter.

The third of the new ones never got into range — it stayed orbiting the south edge, the same zone where something different was about to appear.

Park counted: team at the limit. Five contacts in the last ninety seconds, one agent on his knees, another giving ground. The clearing was holding but wouldn't hold through another wave the same size.

Then a bigger wave arrived.

Park had four exchanges stacked up when the two newcomers came straight at the center. Dodging one put the other on a line for the agent still on his knees. He didn't dodge — he let the inertia flow inward, into his own motion, and threw himself at the first one. His muscles tensed. The ground gave under his push-off foot. He covered ten meters in a time his legs alone didn't account for and went in low, with a body hook that absorbed the field on his knuckles instead of his forearm — the impact shook his arm up to the shoulder, but the gwisin folded over the point of contact. Park rotated his torso and released the energy to the right with a crossing elbow: the gwisin went into the tree to the north. The second one arrived half a second later from the left — Park met it with a front kick straight to the center of mass, the Class-R on his leg activating on impact, and sent it to the ground. The earth gave.

The clearing held. Four minutes of manageable pressure, the team found its rhythm.

Then the south edge of the clearing changed.

The Odem in that direction condensed before there was a visible shape. When the shape arrived, it wasn't anything Park had seen all night. Not human silhouettes, not gwisin. What came out of the south edge had too much volume for the space it occupied: a long torso, shoulders too wide for the legs holding them up, arms ending at the wrong height. No clothes, no recognizable facial features — only the irregular outline of something the island had made with what it had. The edges weren't precise like a gwisin's. They were dense at the center and unraveled outward like smoke that never finished dispersing. Creatures that never quite resembled anything known, swaying in the wind like flames.

Three. At the south edge.

Park's reader buzzed. He pulled it out: Level 3, but the signature didn't match anything from the night. Same number, completely different pattern.

The containment agent went at the first one with the unit. The field activated, the unit read, the agent waited. The reading didn't change. The gwisin protocol was searching for a signature axis these entities didn't have — the unit couldn't find the entry point. The agent tried the alternate axis. Nothing either. Then the entity moved toward him and the agent put the unit between them as an instinctive shield, and when the entity's Odem touched the unit the agent dropped the gear with a short cry and put his hand to his chest. The palm reddened, not from heat — from something the body read as a burn without there being fire. The Class-R on his forearm was translucent to the max, the edge glowing with Odem that wasn't his.

The agent pulled back. The unit on the ground.

Park was already moving south.

Kiki came in from the west before Park reached the flank. She hadn't evaluated from the edge — she'd already read it. She left the case unopened, absorbed the inertia of three running steps, and on the fourth released it upward, multiplied by ten.

The jump wasn't a person's jump.

It cleared the head of the cover agent and landed between him and the three entities at the south edge. At the moment of landing she concentrated mass in her feet. The ground gave, the pressure wave spread through the earth — the entities fell back half a step, the irregular edges contracted by the wave.

The first one recomposed before Kiki lifted her foot off the ground and came at her. Kiki went at it. The fist, mass concentrated, hit the center of the figure and sounded like something breaking — Park felt the wave in his sternum eight meters away. The shape fell back two meters, edges contracted at the point of impact.

Then Kiki opened her right hand and looked at it.

The palm was burning. Not with visible fire — with the same Odem that lived on the entity's surface, active and foreign, burning the point of contact from inside. The Class-R on her right forearm was at maximum trying to compensate. Kiki closed the hand, opened it again, checked that the fingers still answered.

They answered. But she couldn't use that surface again. So she gathered the Odem in her body and wrapped herself in it, like a thin cloak.

The entity rose. Not like a gwisin — in the same motion it had fallen with, the edges recomposing in under a second. It came back at Kiki along the same vector. Kiki absorbed the field with her left forearm instead of taking it with her hand — less contact area, less exposure time — and used the rebound to step back and read the distance. She stored the inertia. The entity didn't yield to distributed impact. It needed maximum concentration at the smallest possible point.

Kiki ran, dodged a sweep from the creature, its arms long and monstrous. She passed beneath its legs, dodged a fist as big as her own head, slipped between its arms, and pushed herself upward with her hands. Her foot loaded with mass, the precise instant and point, inertia as accelerant, and she struck the center of the figure.

This time it stayed on the ground. The edges flickering, not recomposing.

The second one came from her left. Kiki took it on her right forearm — the one already burned by Odem — and the contact was half a second of pain that set her jaw. She absorbed the rebound, used that inertia to turn. Her left elbow reached the center of the figure, mass concentrated, the speed of the turn behind it. The sound was deeper than any exchange of the night. The entity went into the nearest tree, bark splintering.

The third one circled her from the right. Kiki was already turning from the elbow strike — she absorbed the rotation's inertia and redirected it into the kick. Her leg came out faster than its mass should have allowed. The entity hit the ground with such force that the displaced earth carved a trench.

Both creatures got up again.

Kiki went for the closer one first — open palm mimicking a blade, Odem concentrated at the point of contact. The creature attacked, she jumped, and used that instant to drop her arm. The creature ended up split down the middle. Then the second — but the second grabbed her forearm on contact and the Odem burn reached her wrist before Kiki could pull free. One second of contact. Kiki didn't scream — she shook her arm to break the grip, took a step back, hand shaped into a blade the same as before. The creature charged her, Kiki did the same. When the misshapen limbs closed around her, she launched herself forward with inertia. Her arm went through it end to end. The Odem over the Class-R activated to maximum, burning, the creature convulsing. Until it went still.

At the edge of her vision, sector east, something emerged from the forest. A fourth. Not one of the three from the clearing — the Odem it carried was denser, more recent, without the marks of the earlier exchanges. It came at her before Kiki could fully assess it.

This time Kiki met it — she refined the shape of her hand, two fingers instead of the full palm, same technique — crouching, in a single motion that seemed to have no starting point, the small woman launched herself, shot forward like a spring, passed between the creature's legs, and it buckled, collapsing to the ground. She'd cut its legs out from under it. Kiki rolled, like an animal mid-stride, and the instant her feet found a fixed base again, she launched herself. Two turns in the air, and she landed on the creature with such force that it scattered its contents across the ground. Black blood.

Four. The three from the south edge, and this one.

Kiki had both hands at their limit. The concentration the blade technique needed required the hand to work clean, and neither of hers worked clean anymore. She needed her case.

Park arrived from the north.

He'd read the problem in Kiki's movement — the delay between the strike and the concentration, the hands that wouldn't fully close. He didn't go straight for a hit: he went in low, swept the entity's supporting leg with his shin, and when the figure lost its vertical axis he caught its forearm with his left hand and twisted it downward, using the entity's own imbalance to pin it to the ground — not an impact, a lever. The entity ended up pressed under its own weight, no angle to recover its stance while Park held the lock.

Park's left forearm burned at the point of contact.

Kiki saw the angle. The static entity was a different thing — no movement to read, no timing to calibrate. She pressed the two fingers of her right hand together even though the hand wouldn't fully close, concentrated everything she had left into that point, and struck the center of the figure.

Park released the lock at the same moment. The entity hit the ground and didn't get up.

Kiki and Park separated without looking at each other. Park flexed his left forearm — the Class-R still translucent at the edge, the foreign Odem dissipating from the point of contact toward his wrist. Kiki opened and closed her right hand three times. Not all the way, but enough.

The Class-R on both of Kiki's arms was translucent to the max, the muscle and tendon structure visible at the rim of the material.

Park covered the flank while Kiki worked the south — he intercepted two gwisin trying to take the space the team had abandoned when it moved. One gwisin between the two of them, heading for the containment agent: Park read the trajectory, moved to the crossing point, dodged the first advance with a hip turn and answered with an uppercut that closed off the figure's path — deflected it toward where Kiki was finishing her turn. Kiki was already oriented. The fist came out. The gwisin went down.

They hadn't spoken. They hadn't looked at each other.

Ji-ho appeared at the north edge.

Two seconds of assessment. He pointed at Kiki and the cover agent: open palm toward the east, twice. Consolidate. He pointed at the containment agent: same gesture. The team understood — the clearing was holding, the east flank was still taking pressure, the whole team needed to move there.

Park looked south.

The entities at the edge weren't dissolved or contained. They were retreating. Not like something giving ground — like something moving with direction. The irregular edges shifting toward the southern vegetation with a coherence that didn't fit retreating entities. Park pulled out the reader: the highest density wasn't in the clearing. It was further down, in the interior, in the same direction they were moving.

Gwisin flee when they're contained. These weren't fleeing. They were going back.

Park went south.

He crossed the clearing at a jog, reader in hand, the entities' irregular edges still visible among the trees at the edge. He entered the vegetation without stopping — the trail from earlier operations, ground trodden by decades of boots, wide enough for one body. The entities were fifteen meters ahead. Then twelve. Then the terrain dropped and the trunks grew thicker and the light of the clearing disappeared behind him.

The reader climbed. Not linearly — a jump of four points in ten steps, then two steps with no change, then another jump. The density wasn't distributed. It was concentrated at a point further down, and the entities were moving toward that point.

Park followed.

The entities stopped before he did.

It wasn't a gesture of warning or defense — it was a pause, as if something ahead of them had changed and they were responding to it. The irregular edges stabilized. They stood still on the trail, between two old trunks where the ground sloped down toward something Park still couldn't see.

Park stopped eight meters from them. The reader marked beyond the entities. What they were looking for was deeper.

When he looked back the clearing wasn't visible. The sounds of combat arrived muffled, as if from another sector. He hadn't decided to go in — he'd followed the fight and the fight had brought him this far, and now the fight had finished bringing him here and Park was alone with the reader and the still entities and something further down that still had no shape.

---

The substrate hurt.

Not in one place — in several at once, above, in the direction of the noise and the weight and the active Odem that had spent hours crushing what was there. Every time one of its own lost shape, the entity felt it the way cold is felt coming in through a crack: localized, small, real. It didn't think about it. It felt it. The way it had felt every previous closure, every containment, every time something came from above with its devices and its charged bodies and took part of what the place was.

The place was her. The trees and the substrate beneath the trees and the Odem in the roots and the coastline below and her own kind moving above — all of it was part of the same thing, and the same thing was her, and when part of it left, the entity was a little less, with nothing to be done about it.

Then something entered the interior.

Not from above — inward, descending, separating itself from the noise and weight of the clearing. She felt it in the substrate before she felt it in the air. An Odem different from the one carried by the others. The others had clean Odem, active, in use — Odem that came from themselves. This one carried Odem from somewhere else, on top. Something old. Something that weighed the way the depths weigh.

The entity knew it.

Not because she knew where it came from. Because it had the same thing. The same kind of weight, the same kind of age in the Odem. Like two stones from the same river, even though neither knows of the river.

She went still.

The one descending kept coming closer. The substrate carried it, step by step, closer and closer to the point where the ground dropped and the trees were older and the entity had been still longer than the ones coming from above could measure.
