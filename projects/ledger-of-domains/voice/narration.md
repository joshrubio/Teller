# Narration layers: inner voice, elevated narrator, onlookers

Derived from Cap5 (Los Webb) and Cap162 (La marea), then widened with the visual references. Load when a scene uses a narrator outside any head, a chapter built from several places, or thought chains. Inside fights also apply `combat.md` rule 14.

## The three layers
1. **Close POV chain** (default, `style.md`): one head, its senses.
2. **Inner voice:** one short line per beat, set over a face beat, never inside an action chain.
3. **Elevated narrator:** outside any head; knows more than the POV character and may know what comes later. Used in Cap5 over one limited POV, and in Cap162 as a mosaic.

## What Cap5 does (elevated narrator over one limited POV)
- **Dramatic irony by explicit negation:** "Lo que Farid no vio / no supo / no sabía..." returns at each turn of the chapter, always with the reason the POV could not know ("porque no tenía ninguna razón para estar mirando").
- **Two sides, one wall:** what Farid sees is what passes through the shop window, at a distance; what the narrator tells is what happened inside the house. A `---` marks each switch.
- **The protagonist enters unnamed** ("el crío", "el niño"), seen by someone who does not look at him, and the narrator says when he will be named ("Farid nunca supo el nombre del niño hasta mucho después").
- **Evidence, not label:** concrete signs of the gift (pieces ordered by size, the postman's route) and no name for it ("Nadie en esa casa habría llamado a eso un don").
- **The POV's coldness is stated in one clause and not corrected** ("La compasión... no formaba parte de la transacción").
- **Time in plain numbers** ("veintitrés meses, uno antes del plazo").
- **The close is withheld method:** the chapter ends on the decision already made and the how still hidden.

## What Cap162 does (elevated narrator as mosaic)
- **Header line in italics** (date, hour, place), one scene per place, `---` between; about ten sections.
- **Each section opens on a concrete fact and closes on a short hard line,** often a physical image ("se quedó a oscuras", "Seiscientas cuarenta casas lo oyeron a la vez").
- **Context in the narrator's own voice, short, where the stakes need it** ("Costa Rica no tiene ejército. Lo abolió en 1948...").
- **Numbers as pressure:** 221 people, 638 of 640 houses, seven boats; the chapter opens and closes on the same figure.
- **Affect through objects:** a glass of water and a phone; a notebook with one sentence; a wet cassock and shaking hands. Nobody says what they feel.
- **A rule of a power in two plain lines** ("La Omnipresencia no sirve donde uno no ha estado. Ahora había estado.").
- **Groups introduced by one distinguishing detail each,** never by a list of abilities.
- **Hand-off to a close POV at a break,** never mid-scene (Park and Oliver take over when a conversation starts).
- **Understatement at the decisive moment** ("No hubo votación. Nadie la pidió.").

## Rules (both)
- At a turn, one idea per sentence; the long clause-heavy sentence stays for observation.
- Never name the theme or the feeling (`themes/writing-rules.md`).
- The narrator may know the future; he says it once, plainly.
- The narrator never explains what the scene already shows.
- Hand-offs between narrator and a head only at breaks.

## From the visual references (use sparingly; situational patterns in `combat-visual/elevated-narrator.md`)
- Onlookers carry the rule in short cries; the narrator answers in two plain lines.
- Staggered boxes for a doubt, a reconstruction or a verdict; one statement can split across two boxes with an ellipsis.
