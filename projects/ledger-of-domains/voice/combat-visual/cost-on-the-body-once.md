# Cost on the body, once

- Write: a major cost is shown once, in one image, with no comment: a limb gone, a sleeve open at the shoulder, the piece spinning out of the frame, the face still and wide-eyed (no cry). When the cost is a loss, let one line of travel cross the landscape and the body: the scar over the skyline is the reach, the bill and the frame at once, and the reader does the sum. The panel or paragraph division can itself be that line.
- After the single image, carry the loss in silence: it shows in each later frame (an empty sleeve, a missing limb in the frame) and nobody mentions it; he keeps fighting with a grin.
- A single hand in the frame, a fist closed in another, and no mention of the missing arm: the loss is carried, never repeated.
- The fatal cost can be a silent full-page image of the fallen, eyes open, among rubble; no text, no cry.
- Avoid: a wound report; a scream standing in for the image; showing the cost twice.
