# Escalation at the peak

- Write: the answer arrives at the exact moment the protagonist thinks he has won; the new threat fills the frame from below and he is small in a corner. Name it as a ceremony: one long formal line on its own, one short line from whoever calls it. State its rule once, and let the rule make a deadline (it improves with each exposure, so the plan is a clock: end it before it does). Onlookers can say the rule in a question and an answer, plus one line of standing ("that is the family's crown jewel"). Close on the plan failing by a hair and on the smile of the one just hit.
- The tally object can be stopped by hitting it (a stomp on the object that counts the steps); its fall and rattle can close a beat. The apparent win is the opening of the reversal: the chapter can end on a bloodied face with no comment. The countdown can be literal and visible, so the plan is a clock the reader can count.
- Avoid: building a system of summons; the escalation announced beforehand; the reference's entities.
