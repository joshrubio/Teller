# Aftermath

- Write: both alive, walking out of the same dust; footsteps before faces; a dry line each. Then a late reveal of a cost outside the fight (witnesses, a broadcast, the city). Close the beat there, not on rest (`voice/style.md`, chapter cliffhanger rule).
- After a huge payoff, a laconic line from the one who took the blow ("that was exhausting") carries more than a description.
- A closing image can be a single close-up of the one still smiling through blood.
- The last image can be a close-up of the winner, smiling and bloodied, beside a distant wide shot with a slash across a tower; the narrator completes one sentence across two boxes. Do not port the hype caption.
- The winner jokes about his own cost ("it blew me up too, first time"); the loser supplies the criticism ("first use at the last moment is madness"); a third voice does the sum of why it made sense; close on question, "yes", verdict.
- A short farewell from the victor ("you did well; I will not forget you while I live"); the next fighter running toward near-certain death while the narrator says there is no time to mourn; the victor, standing by the body, asks not to have his pleasure spoiled.
- Avoid: explaining the cost; one plain line is enough.
