# Pressure clock

- Write: pressure that cannot be dodged, only paid for: the clock is endurance, and the reader counts what each cycle costs. The defender keeps moving while managing the damage, and that is the cost shown. The attacker reads the weakness out loud (splitting his resource across two jobs makes each one weak, so it only buys time). The defender deduces where the cost really originates, one plain thought that changes every later blow. A pursuit that denies the exit: one short line from the attacker, the body caught mid-air, the floor receiving it. The target reads as a dark shape among many cuts, not wound by wound.
- Two targets at once, the person and what sustains his advantage: hit the second while the clock runs, and let a spectator say the stake with a number.
- A visible, audible tally on the threat (each step a sound), with onlookers saying how many remain; the tally keeps ticking after a clean blow to remind that the clock did not stop.
- The decline is told by the observers (wounds close slower; the same for the enemy).
- Avoid: an unexplained effect (`combat.md` section 6): the pressure needs a physical mechanism; a recovery that resets the fight; copying the reference's names, mechanism or setting.
