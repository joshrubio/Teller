# Two on one: roles and limit

- Write: the protagonist's inner voice lays out the roles in short, equal lines (who attacks, who covers and makes openings, the limit that stops them from using everything at once), then a preference ("I would rather this than being stripped of my options one by one") and an expectation about which of the two will take longer to learn. The enemy corrects the count ("this is not two against one") and the count rises, named as a ceremony by its parts and then one fused name.
- The enemy can spur his own ally with one line that claims it ("you are no longer his shadow, you are mine; show me what you can do") and a closing sound; the ally's hesitation is a cost the enemy pays.
- Avoid: explaining the limit's mechanism; more than one count correction per fight.
