# Declared strike

- Write: a plain rule about the contest said first, then the words before the blow, one clause per line (a vow said aloud, `system/votos.md`, only if the scene already has one). The strike in one clean sentence; its effect read as a straight line through the space. Then dust, a low repeated sound, footsteps announcing who walks out; one line explaining why the defender did not read it. Close by turning the rule back on the striker in one line.
- One word can be the whole declaration: say the name, then the blow.
- The name of a technique can arrive alone on a line before its effect; the payoff blow can be one word.
- A naming can be a long formal title as its own line, preceded by one short line from whoever calls it.
- A recital can be a recovery, not an attack; the onlookers decode it in one line ("he is restoring what he lost").
- The narrator can name the finisher after the damage, over the rubble; the name arrives with the effect.
- A recital can be compressed ("too slow") into fewer words with no loss of force; the cost is time.
- Avoid: explaining the technique; the chant is the stake, not a spell list.
