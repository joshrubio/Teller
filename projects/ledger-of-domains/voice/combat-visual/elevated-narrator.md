# Elevated narrator and inner voice (situational patterns)

General rules live in `combat.md` rule 14, `style.md` (Narration layers) and `narration.md`. This file keeps only the situational patterns.

- Narrator, five uses: the rule before the blow; the state of a balance (equal strength, said once; an asymmetry and why the weaker side acts first); why someone did not read it; the verdict after the decisive blow; the cost outside the fight. Two lines can split one statement with an ellipsis. A chapter can close on the verdict plus one image of its price (a cut across a face).
- Inner voice chains: what he does, what it implies, the risk; or a question, an elimination, a sentence broken across two beats ("which means that surely..."), then one exclamation as the trigger. An allied pair can think in parallel lines that give the odds from two sides. A callback to an earlier fight can explain a move in one line.
- Onlookers can share one deduction across voices (one asks, one completes a broken sentence); one dry aside from a spectator can release the tension, once per fight.
- Onlookers can state a win condition with a number before it happens, then one short line ("he will win"); a tactical reading of the ground from the stands; one tense line when both sides commit at the same moment. A narrator box can state the stake plainly.
- The protagonist's reasoning can run in two steps with a pivot (first the impossible reading, then "no..." and the correct one as a question); the order of events can be the clue.
- A narrator box can state which of the other's blows the defender can blunt and which not, without the mechanism; one onlooker line can name what the protagonist has not done as the clue.
- A long tactical briefing in italics, each clause equal in weight (assessment, limit, preference, expectation); a staggered narrator block for the doubt.
- Avoid: telling what the scene already shows; porting the source's hype captions and sound labels.
