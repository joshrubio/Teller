# Enemy's choices as evidence

- Write: from one small number (one exposure) and one choice that does not fit (the enemy keeps attacking instead of preparing), the protagonist deduces how the rule works, in two or three short steps; one clause is cut off mid-sentence and picked up in the next box ("it is solid proof that..."). Then the cost in time against the height of the enemy's guard, and the forced conclusion.
- The cost of the last blow, said as a measurable fact (what was lost taking it head-on), is used to estimate the risk of the next, by the one who took it; the inference is hedged ("probably some kind of vow"; our vow, `system/votos.md`, only if the scene already has one).
- The winner can buy a pattern by letting his ally be hurt or taught, because the data was the goal, not the damage.
- Avoid: a conclusion that needs the reference's mechanics; a deduction the earlier beats did not feed.
