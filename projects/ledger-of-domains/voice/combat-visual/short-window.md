# Short window

- Write: the decisive blow lands in a gap too short to see. Order: onlookers' two readings ("did he get ahead of it?" / "maybe, just maybe"), then two narrator lines explaining the gap (the other was spending his attention or resource elsewhere, recovering or holding something up; an interval counted in hundredths), then the blow declared in one line, then the body taking it. After the blow, the winner does not close: one plain line lists the next targets and measures them against an ally's earlier brush with death. What was spent earlier is what opens the gap. The window can also close the other way: the plan is to finish it before it improves, then the next line says it already did.
- Avoid: showing the window itself; a gap the earlier beats did not plant; the reference's technique names or mechanism.
