## Anke = Caso 3 (canon)

Voss has three documented Registry cases across 80 years. The third: worked for Geneva 11 years, retired ~2014 for undocumented medical reasons. Voss knew this person personally. Kaspar observed Anke for eleven years — the numbers match.

She is that case. **Voss knows the outcome. Oliver does not.**

**Corrected 2026-07-10:** the note originally implied Oliver would tell Voss "everything" about the plaza and Anke shortly after arriving at the new location. That conversation no longer happens at all — see `relations/conflicts.md` and LoD Cap 37. Oliver deliberately withholds it, indefinitely, choosing to hold what he knows for the next time he finds Kaspar himself rather than hand it to Voss. Voss still doesn't know that Oliver knows her name. That gap stays open — no forced resolution window anymore.

## Personality

Dry, oblique, tests people instead of answering them directly — the half-fragment she gives Oliver in Cap 57 ("todo Domain tiene un componente de esto") is her default register, not an exception. Calm authority in front of a room; doesn't raise her voice to be heard. Physical tell, unexplained until Cap 60's reveal: orients toward active Odem, not toward sound or the face people expect her to turn to — she'll "look" at whoever's Domain just flared, not at whoever's speaking, and turns her head a fraction late when someone silent enters a room. Reads as unsettling before it reads as blind. Once Cap 60 explains the mechanism, it recontextualizes every one of these small tells the reader has already seen. Respects Reber (`characters/anton-reber.md`) as one of the few people at San Gotardo who's paid a cost comparable to hers, though the two are not warm with each other. Cites the center's director (working name **Kessler**, never appearing on-page in Libro 2) with the same institutional weight Reber does — a ceiling neither of them can override.

## Narrative function

Anke is the mirror Oliver cannot read directly. The Registry leaves no signature when inactive — he cannot copy anyone, cannot see another carrier's trajectory in real time. All he has is Kaspar's word — until Libro 2, where she appears in person.

**Why she matters in Libro 2:** Oliver made the decision to stay without knowing what that means long-term. Anke is the only evidence it happened before. Since Oliver no longer plans to tell Voss what he knows, this encounter is not a payoff of the "Sion" promise (that thread stays closed per Cap 37 rewrite) — it's a new, separate thread: Voss brings her to San Gotardo as an informal precedent case while the formal methodology is stuck in Geneva (line C). Short — scenes folded inside línea A chapters, not a dedicated line of its own.

## Libro 2 arc — 9 short scenes inside línea A chapters (Cap 45, 50, 53, 56, 59, 60, 62, 63, 80 — numeración absoluta, actualizada tras la reestructura del 2026-07-22)

<!-- canonical -->
- **Cap 45 (public debut, retimed from original Cap 54):** first physical appearance, much earlier than originally planned — teaches a theory-practice class on Odem control, tests candidates one by one by subjugating them with raw Odem pressure. Her blindness is visually obvious from the moment she enters, which makes the demonstration land stranger: she never once seems lost or hesitant navigating the room or singling out students. Oliver's group (behind on the training curve) notices this class is specifically for the ones lagging, and starts suspecting there's more to this instructor than the room lets on — both from how she talks and from how impossibly well she moves for someone who can't see. No private one-on-one yet — pure public first impression, long runway before the real reveal (Cap 62-63).
- **Cap 50 (intercalada, recurring):** takes all three groups (Valais, Dax, Johanna) to San Gotardo's physical archive vault for a three-way reconstruction race using real redacted 1727-era case files, instead of lecturing directly — second data point feeding the group's suspicion (she directs the whole exercise without ever needing to look at a document herself), still nothing confirmed.
- **Cap 53 (intercalada, retimed — was the 1755 Lisboa lore lesson, cut for feeling repetitive with Cap50; replaced same day):** pulls Dara aside privately after the underwater test, asks her to activate her Doble Domain "just to look at it" — no exercise justification, no explanation given. Dara doesn't ask the obvious question (why would someone who can't see want to *look*) out loud, but the reader feels it. Third data point feeding the group's suspicion, deepened rather than resolved — payoff/continuation reserved for Cap56, the next línea A chapter (Cap54-55 are línea D/C).
- **Cap 56 (intercalada, retimed — was "arrival," now first private conversation):** first one-on-one with Oliver, brief. She's reading him already — Oliver doesn't notice, only a vague sense something's off in how she answers.
- **Cap 59 (intercalada):** second private conversation. She references something about his recent training she'd have no casual way of knowing. Oliver files it away, unsettled, says nothing yet.
- **Cap 60 (intercalada):** Oliver confronts her directly, asks if she's reading him. She neither confirms nor fully denies — gives him a half-fragment ("todo Domain tiene un componente de esto") without explaining, as if testing whether he'll get there himself. He recalls Voss's birthday game (LoD Cap 24) and Pier's line ("el Odem no empieza en la piel").
- **Cap 62 (cierre):** full reveal. Confirms she's been reading him and why — the same universal principle behind every Domain (concentrating Odem in one body part sharpens it locally: Yusuf's hands/forearms/ribs, Park's point of contact) applied, for the first time on record, to a sense organ: her own eyes. Starts teaching him.
- **Cap 63 (cierre, deliberate thematic echo with the chapter title "Lo que cuesta"):** first real attempt — fails at first, like his early failed copies. Then it clicks: his sight stops being abstract data (contornos, contraste) and becomes genuinely sensory — color, shape, movement, the first time he actually *sees* Odem instead of reading it. See `system/registro.md` for the full mechanical writeup. She tells him the cost plainly: eleven years using both eyes constantly with no idea there was a cheaper way; discovered at year 8 that alternating one eye at a time reduced the strain hugely — by then the damage was already permanent. Retired at year 11. Teaches Oliver the alternating discipline from day one, the safeguard she never had.
- **Cap 80 (cierre of the thread):** farewell. Her informal consulting work ends, she returns to El Quai. Brief, no forced sentimentality. Oliver decides what to do with what he's learned — tells neither the group nor Voss.

## Writing rule

<!-- canonical -->
Her *personal history before AMAT* and her *final fate after Libro 2* stay unrevealed — no exposition dump on either. What Libro 2 DOES reveal, on-page, in fragments across the three scenes above: the eleven years as evaluator, the ocular concentration technique, the vision cost and its timeline (year 8 discovery, year 11 retirement), and the technique itself. That's the payoff for this book. Kaspar's fuller version of who she was (if it ever comes) stays reserved for a later book.
